Special Containment Procedures

All known storage media containing {{SCP_ID}} are to be retained by Site-41 Media Analysis. Publicly accessible copies are to be removed when discovered and, where practical, replaced with a separately produced non-anomalous recreation based on station transcripts, production records, and contemporary still photographs.

Foundation web crawlers are to flag uploads containing source-file hashes, metadata, audio segments, or visual signatures associated with any documented version of {{SCP_ID}}. Signature databases are to be updated following each authorized playback. Automated systems are not to decode more than thirty consecutive frames from a suspected copy before the file has been quarantined for authorized review.

Individuals publicly claiming that {{SCP_ID}} has changed between viewings are to be identified and monitored. Direct intervention is not required unless such claims attract sustained public attention or result in independent preservation efforts.

Playback of {{SCP_ID}} requires Level-3 authorization from the project lead. Simultaneous playbacks are prohibited. Each playback is to be entered into the Site-41 viewing log with the following information:

  • Personnel present;
  • Start and stop times;
  • Total duration decoded;
  • Equipment used;
  • Significant deviations from the previous documented version.

For recordkeeping purposes, a playback event is defined as a single decoding session in which more than thirty consecutive frames of any moving-image instance of {{SCP_ID}} are rendered. Playback numbers used in this document refer only to documented Bellweather Media Archive and Foundation events and should not be interpreted as the total number of times {{SCP_ID}} has been viewed.

All playback output is to be routed through an isolated capture system. Every decoded frame is to be exported simultaneously as a separate lossless still image and written to non-rewritable storage. Audio is to be preserved separately as an uncompressed waveform and transcript. Moving-image recordings are not to be considered reliable archival evidence.

No more than twenty intentional playbacks may be conducted within any twenty-four-hour period without written approval from the project lead and Site-41 administration.

No individual is to be detained, arrested, publicly accused, or otherwise subjected to coercive action solely on the basis of information obtained from {{SCP_ID}}. Searches and evidence-recovery operations based on otherwise uncorroborated {{SCP_ID}} content require written approval from the project lead and Site-41 administration. Evidence recovered through such operations is to be evaluated independently of {{SCP_ID}} before any action is taken against an identified person.

Access to Record {{SCP_NUMBER}}-9,004-A is restricted to assigned project personnel and authorized members of the Memetics Department. All access is to be logged. Further testing involving Record {{SCP_NUMBER}}-9,004-A is suspended.

Description

{{SCP_ID}} is a local television news segment originally broadcast by KKMN-TV, a former independent station serving Redwick, California, on June 14, 2003.

The earliest documented version had a duration of 2 minutes, 17 seconds and concerned the reopening of the Redwick Municipal Footbridge following six months of repair work. It consisted primarily of footage recorded during the reopening ceremony, including brief remarks from Mayor Daniel Corbett, interviews with two local residents, and a closing statement by KKMN reporter Allison Price.

No anomalous event is known to have occurred during the original ceremony.

{{SCP_ID}} was discovered in 2021 after employees of Bellweather Media Archive, a private audiovisual preservation company, uploaded a digitized copy as part of a collection of archived KKMN broadcasts. Several viewers subsequently claimed that the segment differed from versions they had previously seen.

Initial disagreements concerned minor details, including camera position, the wording of Price’s closing statement, and the presence or absence of individual spectators. Similar disagreements were later reported among Bellweather employees actively reviewing the source file.

The Foundation acquired the file, its storage media, and all associated restoration materials following automated detection of online discussion concerning apparently inconsistent archival footage.

The recovered source file and all verified direct duplicates remain bitwise identical, and their cryptographic hashes do not change as a result of playback.

Nevertheless, {{SCP_ID}} does not produce identical audiovisual content during successive playback events.

Alterations are generally cumulative. Newly introduced footage, dialogue, individuals, objects, and environmental details remain present in later versions. The total duration of {{SCP_ID}} has consequently increased with repeated playback.

Attempts to preserve a superseded version through duplication, transcoding, screen capture, analog transfer, external-camera capture, or any other moving-image recording method have failed. Any moving-image copy produced through these methods displays the most recent version when played, regardless of when the copy was created.

Still images are unaffected. A frame exported during an earlier playback continues to depict the version visible at the moment of capture.

It remains unknown whether {{SCP_ID}} is revealing previously unrecorded events, generating internally consistent additions to the historical record, or modifying the events it depicts retroactively.

Discovery

Bellweather Media Archive obtained approximately 1,800 hours of KKMN footage in 2019 following the liquidation of the station’s former parent company. {{SCP_ID}} was digitized in November 2020 and viewed repeatedly during restoration due to intermittent audio degradation near the end of the segment.

Bellweather records document 186 playback events before public release. No employee identified any content as anomalous during those events.

The first specific discrepancy investigated as a possible alteration was documented during Playback 201, when Analyst K. Weller noted that Mayor Corbett blinked twice at 00:43.

A subsequent review of Bellweather restoration records located the following note from Playback 37:

Minor eyelid flutter on Corbett at 00:43. Likely source artifact. No restoration needed.

The Bellweather technician responsible for the note stated that they distinctly remembered observing a single blink. No still image from Playback 37 existed, and the discrepancy could not be independently confirmed.

Playback 202 again showed two complete blinks.

Limited controlled playback was authorized.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.1 — Selected Playback Changes

The following changes are considered representative.

Playback 214

Total duration increased by 0.8 seconds.

Mayor Corbett hesitates before saying, “Good morning.”

The hesitation persisted in all subsequent versions.

Playback 263

An unidentified child appears behind Corbett for approximately four seconds.

The child is not visible in still photographs taken during the original ceremony and has not been identified.

Playback 411

During Price’s interview with resident Samuel Vey, the camera pans approximately eight degrees farther to the left.

The movement reveals three additional spectators and part of a parked vehicle previously outside the frame. The alteration adds 1.4 seconds to the recording.

The spectators remain unidentified.

Playback 702

Price asks Vey a second question concerning the bridge’s closure.

Vey responds that he has been walking across the bridge “since before they put the railing up.”

Municipal records indicate that the bridge and its railing were completed in 1978. Vey was born in 1981.

Vey died in 2014. Interviews with surviving relatives produced no explanation for the statement.

Playback 1,106

Six seconds of crowd footage appear between two frames that were consecutive during Playback 1,105.

Ambient audio continues naturally through the inserted interval, without an audible edit or discontinuity.

Playback 1,944

A marked patrol vehicle appears on a maintenance road approximately 70 m north of the bridge.

The vehicle was identified as belonging to Redwick County Sheriff’s Deputy Martin Dyer. Dispatch records place Dyer and the vehicle approximately 11 km south of the ceremony at the time the footage was recorded.

Dyer was reported missing on the evening of June 14, 2003. His vehicle was recovered abandoned two days later, 26 km west of Redwick.

In {{SCP_ID}}, the vehicle is visible turning onto a disused municipal access road. Archived maps indicate that the road terminated at a drainage culvert concealed by later construction.

A Foundation search of the culvert in 2024 recovered Dyer’s remains.

No evidence recovered from the culvert established how Dyer’s remains came to be there or how his vehicle entered the area depicted by {{SCP_ID}}.

Following this discovery, continued playback was authorized on the possibility that additional alterations might reveal otherwise unavailable information concerning unresolved criminal cases, missing persons, or other historical events.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.2 — Unidentified Individual

Beginning with Playback 2,831, observers reported intermittent appearances of an unidentified adult male within {{SCP_ID}}.

The individual has been designated {{SCP_ID}}-1.

Unlike all other documented additions, {{SCP_ID}}-1 does not persist between playback events.

{{SCP_ID}}-1 is consistently described as a light-skinned male approximately 35 to 50 years of age, of average height and build, with dark hair and no distinctive clothing or visible physical features.

All personnel who have reported seeing {{SCP_ID}}-1 during playback described a strong impression that they had encountered him previously. No observer successfully identified the source of that impression.

The first documented appearance occurred during Playback 2,831. Research Assistant Molina reported seeing {{SCP_ID}}-1 at the rear of a newly introduced crowd shot, partially obscured by Allison Price.

Molina completed the playback and requested immediate review.

During Playback 2,832, the crowd shot remained present.

{{SCP_ID}}-1 did not.

Molina’s report was attributed to fatigue.

The second reported appearance occurred during Playback 3,407 and was observed independently by two personnel. Both described {{SCP_ID}}-1 standing near the northern bridge entrance. Both stated that he appeared to be watching the ceremony rather than the camera.

Playback 3,408 contained no such individual.

Following a third confirmed appearance, full-frame still extraction became mandatory during all testing.

As of Playback 6,112, appearances of {{SCP_ID}}-1 had been reported during fourteen playback events.

Every reported appearance occurred within footage introduced during the same playback event. {{SCP_ID}}-1 has never been observed in material already documented in an earlier version.

Eleven of these events occurred after the introduction of full-frame extraction. In each case, the reported time and location were recorded immediately after playback. Review of the corresponding still frames showed no individual at the reported location.

During three events, multiple observers independently recorded timestamps differing by less than 0.4 seconds.

No objective image of {{SCP_ID}}-1 was recovered.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.3 — Playback 6,803

Playback 6,803 increased the total duration of {{SCP_ID}} to 19 minutes, 41 seconds.

Most of the newly introduced material occurred before and after the original broadcast segment.

The recording now begins approximately seven minutes before Price’s scheduled introduction. During this period, KKMN personnel prepare equipment, speak with attendees, and wait for the ceremony to begin.

Approximately four minutes of footage following the original conclusion are also present. Price and the camera operator can be seen packing equipment and leaving the immediate area.

No event depicted within the additional material is independently anomalous.

During post-playback review, Analyst Weller entered the following note:

{{SCP_ID}} is no longer adding to the broadcast. It is filling in the time around it.

Testing was suspended for twelve days while the project’s stated purpose and evidentiary value were reviewed.

Controlled playback subsequently resumed.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.4 — Playback 8,219

At Playback 8,219, {{SCP_ID}} reached a duration of 47 minutes, 12 seconds.

New footage extended substantially beyond the period during which KKMN personnel were present.

For the first time, the recording continued after Price, the camera operator, and all identified attendees had left the bridge.

The viewpoint remained stationary at the approximate location occupied by the KKMN camera during the ceremony.

Station equipment records indicate that the camera was loaded into the KKMN vehicle at 10:58 and removed from the area with the production crew. {{SCP_ID}} nevertheless continues to depict the bridge from the same viewpoint until 11:26.

At 11:09, a sedan passes through the foreground on a service road adjacent to the bridge.

The vehicle’s license plate was registered to a sedan reported stolen on June 12, 2003. The vehicle was recovered on June 16. No fingerprints or biological material attributable to its driver were recovered.

The driver has not been identified.

Further playback was authorized.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.5 — Playback 9,004

Between Playbacks 8,219 and 9,004, {{SCP_ID}} continued to increase in duration. New material appeared both before the arrival of KKMN personnel and after their departure.

During Playback 9,004, the recording had a total duration of 1 hour, 26 minutes, 18 seconds.

At 01:13:26, the viewpoint begins a slow pan toward an area approximately 30 m north of the bridge.

The movement is smooth and consistent with deliberate operation of a tripod-mounted camera.

Post-playback review identified {{SCP_ID}}-1 in a single exported frame at the extreme right edge of the image. The preceding and following frames depict the same area without him.

During Playback 9,005, the pan remained present.

{{SCP_ID}}-1 did not.

The extracted frame was designated Record {{SCP_NUMBER}}-9,004-A. Site-41 Imaging determined it to be an unaltered output of the capture system. It is the first objective visual record of {{SCP_ID}}-1 within {{SCP_ID}}.

All twenty-three project personnel shown the complete image reported the same unplaceable sense of familiarity associated with previous appearances of {{SCP_ID}}-1.

None could identify him.

The image was compared against records of KKMN employees, ceremony attendees, municipal personnel, missing persons, Foundation staff, and known anomalous individuals. No match was identified.

To determine whether recognition depended on the bridge or other contextual details, the Memetics Department prepared a cropped version containing only {{SCP_ID}}-1’s face and upper torso. The crop was inserted into a randomized array with twenty-four control photographs matched for resolution, image quality, and the apparent age, sex, complexion, and build of the subjects.

Thirty-two Site-41 employees with no assignment to {{SCP_ID}} participated in a blind recognition trial. Participants were instructed to indicate whether any subject in the array appeared familiar and, if so, to state where they believed they had seen that person. They were not informed that the array contained anomalous material or that any image originated from a recording.

Thirty-one participants reported no familiarity with any subject.

Archivist Miriam Cole selected the image of {{SCP_ID}}-1 immediately.

Cole stated:

“That’s the man from the bridge video.”

Cole had never viewed {{SCP_ID}}. She had no documented access to project materials, Bellweather records, KKMN footage, or Record {{SCP_NUMBER}}-9,004-A. Neither the word bridge nor the word video appeared in her briefing or in the test materials.

Cole was informed of these facts.

She confirmed that she had no prior knowledge of the project.

When asked why she had used the phrase “the bridge video,” Cole did not respond for approximately twelve seconds.

She then stated:

“I don’t know. I thought that was what everyone called it.”

Further recognition testing was suspended, and access to Record {{SCP_NUMBER}}-9,004-A was restricted pending additional memetic review.

Playback authorization remains active.
