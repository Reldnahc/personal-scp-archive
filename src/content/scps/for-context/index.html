NOTICE FROM THE RECORDS AND INFORMATION SECURITY ADMINISTRATION

All prior documentation concerning {{SCP_ID}} has been withdrawn from circulation.

This file contains all information presently considered necessary for containment. Requests to restore omitted material will be denied.

Special Containment Procedures

{{SCP_ID}} is stored in Secure Item Locker 14-B at Site-19. It is not to be removed except as necessary to transfer it to an equivalent locker following damage to the current unit. No further testing is authorized.

Foundation personnel are not to intentionally create or modify any additional record whose subject is {{SCP_ID}}. This prohibition includes annotations, summaries, indexes, cross-references, revision notes, meeting minutes, correspondence, and requests to amend this file.

The present file constitutes the sole authorized record concerning {{SCP_ID}}. It is to remain write-protected.

No explanation for this exception is to be added.

All superseded documentation concerning {{SCP_ID}} is held in offline archival quarantine and is not to be accessed.

Personnel who believe that additional historical, operational, biographical, administrative, technical, or classificatory context would improve this document are to close the file immediately and take no further action.

Description

{{SCP_ID}} is a red desktop stapler of conventional manufacture measuring 18.2 cm in length.

No anomalous physical properties have been identified. When last tested in 1983, {{SCP_ID}} functioned as an ordinary stapler.

The anomaly associated with {{SCP_ID}} manifests when a person intentionally creates or modifies a record intended to document it. The effect is independent of medium and does not require physical proximity to the object.

Affected personnel develop an escalating conviction that additional contextual information is necessary for the record to be accurate, complete, or comprehensible. The effect does not manifest as disorganized or obviously irrelevant writing. Additions are grammatically coherent, locally relevant, and ordinarily supported by existing Foundation records.

Each addition, however, introduces further persons, events, policies, organizations, or terminology which appear to require explanation. Attempts to shorten affected documentation produce the same response: editors conclude that material marked for removal is necessary to understand another portion of the record.

For example, an attempt to document {{SCP_ID}}’s acquisition may identify the property on which it was found. Establishing the property’s relevance may require its ownership history, including a former Foundation front company. Explaining that company may then require documentation of the operation for which it was created, the personnel involved, and associated anomalies.

To affected personnel, no individual addition appears expendable.

Informal discussion of {{SCP_ID}} is unaffected unless one or more participants intend for the discussion to be recorded, transcribed, or incorporated into documentation concerning it.

No reliable record identifies when {{SCP_ID}} received its designation or what observation first prompted its classification as anomalous. Attempts to resolve either question are prohibited.

Archival Note

Before the adoption of the current procedures, documentation concerning {{SCP_ID}} underwent repeated expansion and reorganization from at least 1983 until its withdrawal in 2024. Every substantial attempt to index, reconcile, or shorten the file increased its size.

The withdrawn archive contained, among other material:

  • 6,418 appendices;
  • 31,772 interview transcripts;
  • three separate glossaries, including one defining terms used by another glossary;
  • eleven competing timelines of {{SCP_ID}}’s acquisition;
  • complete operational histories for three Mobile Task Forces mentioned only once elsewhere in the file;
  • seven parallel classification schemes, none of which altered handling requirements;
  • a 412-page Ethics Committee memorandum concerning whether deletion of previous revisions constituted improper destruction of institutional history; and
  • an incomplete review of whether {{SCP_ID}} should be reclassified as Thaumiel.

{{SCP_ID}} was provisionally designated Apollyon during Revision 3,882 on the grounds that “complete documentary containment is no longer achievable.”

The object remained in Locker 14-B.

At the time of withdrawal, the active repository occupied 18.7 terabytes, excluding backups and duplicate revisions.

All stored content was plain text.

Excerpt from Records Reduction Meeting {{SCP_NUMBER}}-611

The following exchange occurred during a 2024 attempt to replace the existing {{SCP_ID}} file with a shortened version.

Dr. Talbot: Why is there a forty-three-page biography of President Eisenhower in this document?

Archivist Chen: It explains the procurement policy.

Dr. Talbot: Why do I need to understand 1950s procurement policy to contain a stapler?

Archivist Chen: You don’t.

Dr. Talbot: Delete it.

Archivist Chen: I can’t.

Dr. Talbot: Why?

Archivist Chen: Because then the section on standardized office equipment doesn’t make sense.

Dr. Talbot: Delete that too.

Archivist Chen: Then you won’t understand why Site-19 stocked this model.

Dr. Talbot: I don’t need to know why Site-19 stocked this model.

[Eight-second pause.]

Archivist Chen: That’s what I said yesterday.

Dr. Talbot: What changed?

Archivist Chen: I read the procurement section.

Dr. Talbot: Stop reading it.

Archivist Chen: I’m supposed to decide what to delete.

Dr. Talbot: Fine. Delete everything before the containment procedures.

Archivist Chen: We tried that in Revision 4,091.

Dr. Talbot: What happened?

Archivist Chen: We had to add a note explaining why the material had been removed.

Dr. Talbot: How long was the note?

Archivist Chen: Initially?

Dr. Talbot: Chen.

Archivist Chen: Six pages.

Dr. Talbot: Initially.

Archivist Chen: The final version was nine hundred and eleven pages.

[Fourteen seconds of silence.]

Dr. Talbot: We’re starting over.

Archivist Chen: From which version?

Dr. Talbot: None of them. New document. No appendices. What do we actually need to know?

Archivist Chen: It’s a stapler.

Dr. Talbot: Good.

Archivist Chen: In a locker.

Dr. Talbot: Good.

Archivist Chen: And documenting it causes—

Dr. Talbot: Stop.

Archivist Chen: Right.

Dr. Talbot: That’s the file.

Archivist Chen: We should probably explain why.

Dr. Talbot: Get out.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.1

Following Meeting {{SCP_NUMBER}}-611, all previous {{SCP_ID}} documentation was removed from the active Foundation database and placed in archival quarantine. The current file was drafted under a fixed length limit and placed under permanent write protection immediately following approval.

During final review, twenty-seven personnel submitted requests to restore omitted information. The stated reasons included clarification of {{SCP_ID}}’s manufacturing date, explanation of the term “desktop stapler,” preservation of its complete acquisition history, correction of allegedly misleading simplifications, inclusion of previous classification debates, and documentation of the decision to omit previous classification debates.

All requests were denied.

No physical containment failure involving {{SCP_ID}} has ever occurred.

As of 2026, {{SCP_ID}} remains in Locker 14-B.

It has not been used since 1983.
