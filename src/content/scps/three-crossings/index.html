Former Object Class: Euclid

Special Containment Procedures

All known artifacts, photographs, casts, facsimiles, translations, field notes, and experimental records capable of enabling reconstruction of {{SCP_ID}} are to be retained by the Site-41 Historical Anomalies Archive.

Foundation personnel embedded within archaeological institutions, museums, antiquities markets, and relevant academic publishers are to monitor material associated with the ███████ ritual corpus. Newly recovered sources are to be acquired or rendered incomplete before public release when they contain previously undocumented procedural information.

Publications containing partial descriptions of the historical rites need not be withdrawn unless the information available across one or more sources is sufficient to reconstruct all known activation requirements.

Access to the minimum {{SCP_ID}} procedure is restricted to assigned project personnel. Experimental activation requires approval from the project lead and may be performed only on mechanically isolated test assemblies capable of being loaded to failure without human presence.

Any mechanical system known or strongly suspected to have undergone {{SCP_ID}} activation is to be designated pending and removed from service. Where practical, the system is to be subjected remotely to controlled mechanical loading until one qualifying failure occurs. Large structures that cannot be tested safely are to be relieved of operational and temporary loads to the greatest extent practicable, closed to human use, and dismantled remotely where possible.

Components recovered from a pending system dismantled before a qualifying failure are not to be returned to service. Such components are to be destructively processed or retained indefinitely under the authority of the project lead.

Foundation maintenance areas containing containment-critical machinery are to remain free of standing reflective liquid while metallic tools or equipment are in use. Equivalent requirements have been introduced into selected civilian aviation, construction, transportation, and industrial-maintenance standards under conventional occupational-safety justifications.

Suspected civilian activations are to be investigated only when sufficient pre-failure evidence survives to reconstruct the event. A mechanical failure is not to be classified as anomalous solely because no adequate conventional cause has been identified.

No physical test is known to distinguish a pending system from an unaffected system before failure occurs.

Description

{{SCP_ID}} is a reproducible anomalous process affecting mechanical systems and load-bearing structures.

The Foundation has reproduced activation only when all of the following conditions are present:

  1. A human operator must directly observe the target reflected in a continuous free surface of liquid.
  2. While keeping the reflection in view, the operator must move a rigid metallic object completely through the apparent image of the target three times. The object must contact the liquid during each crossing.
  3. Within 120 seconds of the third crossing, the liquid must cease to exist as a continuous reflective surface.

For the purposes of this document, a crossing begins outside the apparent boundary of the reflected target, passes through it, and exits beyond the opposite boundary. Reversing direction may constitute a separate crossing.

The operator’s viewpoint determines what is targeted. A reflection visible from another position, through a camera, or only in recorded footage does not satisfy the first requirement.

The liquid may be removed by spilling, drainage, pumping, absorption, evaporation, or comparable means. Rippling or temporarily disturbing the surface does not complete activation if the reflective surface subsequently reforms.

The operator need not intend to damage the target, understand the procedure, or recognize the reflected object as mechanically significant.

No immediate alteration follows activation. Dimensional inspection, radiography, acoustic testing, materials analysis, and controlled disassembly have identified no consistent physical difference between activated systems and untreated controls.

The affected system instead enters a persistent state designated pending.

During a later period of mechanical loading, the affected system will undergo one qualifying failure through a mechanism physically possible under the conditions present.

For the purposes of this document, a qualifying failure is a fracture, seizure, separation, loss of retention, or loss of load-bearing capacity sufficient to impair the target’s intended mechanical function or make continued operation unsafe. Cosmetic damage, the intended release of a sacrificial safety device, and damage caused solely by deliberate disassembly do not qualify.

The failure mechanism remains consistent with the target’s materials, geometry, loading, temperature, vibration, wear, and other relevant physical properties.

Observed failures have included:

  • brittle fracture;
  • fatigue fracture;
  • fastener failure;
  • bearing seizure;
  • loss of mechanical retention;
  • weld cracking;
  • cable separation; and
  • structural buckling.

The resulting damage is not anomalous in isolation. Post-failure examination has always identified a conventional mechanism sufficient to explain how the component failed.

A pre-existing defect is not required. New and independently inspected components have failed during operation within rated limits when an otherwise possible failure pathway existed. Existing damage, excessive loading, poor maintenance, fatigue, and manufacturing defects substantially reduce the interval between activation and failure.

Because anomalous and conventional failures are physically indistinguishable, resolution cannot be confirmed in an individual system. In matched trials, however, activated cohorts exhibit a reproducible shift in the distribution of cycles or load required to reach the first qualifying failure relative to untreated controls. After the first qualifying failure, repaired assemblies return to control behavior unless activated again; no continuing elevation in failure rate has been observed.

The first qualifying failure following activation is therefore treated as resolution of the pending state, even when an independently sufficient conventional cause for that failure exists.

Applying the procedure again to systems already designated pending has not measurably altered their first-failure distributions.

No maximum dormant interval has been established while a pending target remains out of service. One Foundation assembly remained isolated from operational and test loading for twenty-seven months and failed during its first subsequent series of load cycles.

No pending test assembly has undergone a qualifying failure while isolated from operational, test, impact, and transport loading. Static self-weight, residual stress, and ordinary thermal variation have not been sufficient in Foundation trials.

Foundation personnel can reliably target discrete machines and structures. Restricting the portion of the target visible in reflection can reduce the region in which failure later occurs, but no experiment has reliably selected an individual component.

Several historical accounts describe substantially greater precision. Project personnel consider these claims products of selective preservation, retrospective interpretation, and incomplete archaeological evidence.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-1: Discovery

{{SCP_ID}} was identified during Foundation review of material excavated from the ruins of the Late Bronze Age city of ███████ in southeastern Anatolia.

Between 2019 and 2022, archaeologists recovered eleven fragmentary tablets describing ceremonies performed before sieges, quarrying operations, prisoner releases, and naval engagements.

The ceremonies were initially believed to represent unrelated religious practices. Different texts invoked different deities, required different sacrificial animals, and prescribed different prayers, garments, implements, offerings, directions, and times of day.

Foundation archaeological consultant Dr. Selin Karaca identified one recurring sequence.

In each sufficiently complete account:

  • liquid was collected in a shallow vessel;
  • the intended object was viewed in the liquid’s surface;
  • a metal implement was moved through the reflection; and
  • the vessel was subsequently overturned or emptied.

The most complete tablet describes a ceremony performed before an assault on the fortified city of ██████. The relevant passage has been translated as follows:

At first light, take the blood of the horned ram.

Set the black bowl where the western gate lies within it.

Do not look upon the gate except in the blood.

Give the god his three wounds.

Empty the bowl upon the earth.

Before the third sunset, the gate will betray those who trust it.

Contemporary accounts from ██████ state that the western gate became unusable during the subsequent siege following failure of a hinge assembly.

The event was not initially considered anomalous.

Further review identified eight ceremonies followed by independently documented mechanical failures. The affected systems included gates, ship rigging, quarry equipment, restraints, and wheeled military machinery.

The surviving accounts span approximately six centuries.

Foundation reconstruction was authorized.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-2: Reconstruction

A full reconstruction of the ██████ gate ceremony was performed using a purpose-built wooden gate mounted on modern steel hardware.

All components were independently inspected and operated for 10,000 cycles before testing without measurable damage.

An adult horned ram was slaughtered according to the translated procedure. Its blood was collected in a bronze basin reconstructed from archaeological measurements.

The operator positioned the basin so that the gate appeared in the blood’s surface, moved a replica bronze implement through the prescribed figure while reciting the surviving invocation, and overturned the basin.

No immediate change was detected.

Nine hours later, the lower hinge pin fractured during routine operation.

Metallurgical examination identified brittle fracture originating from a microscopic inclusion within the steel. The inclusion was within accepted manufacturing tolerances and had not been detected during pre-test inspection.

The result was classified as inconclusive.

Two subsequent reconstructions produced failure of an upper hinge fastener and seizure of a roller bearing, respectively.

Controlled reduction testing was then conducted.

Because a pending state cannot be detected before failure, individual assemblies were not classified as positive or negative. Each modification was tested across matched cohorts of standardized assemblies subjected to identical loading schedules. In the table below, Positive indicates a reproducible shift in the distribution of cycles or load required to reach the first qualifying failure, consistent with full-procedure cohorts and measurably distinct from untreated controls. Negative indicates no measurable difference from untreated controls within the limits of the protocol and does not establish that activation is impossible under all conditions.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-3: Accidental Activation

On 2024-06-18, Test Assembly 12 experienced an {{SCP_ID}}-consistent failure despite not having been assigned to an activation trial.

Test Assembly 12 was a steel lifting frame used to evaluate control specimens. It had undergone inspection four days earlier and contained no components previously exposed to {{SCP_ID}} testing.

At 10:31, a hydraulic fitting developed a minor leak during unrelated operation.

Technician Aaron Feld knelt beside the assembly to contain the spill. A shallow pool of hydraulic fluid had formed on the sealed laboratory floor.

Security footage shows Feld moving a steel-handled floor scraper through the pool three times while retrieving an absorbent barrier from beneath the frame. The upper pulley assembly was reflected in the fluid from Feld’s position.

Feld later confirmed that he could see the frame in the surface of the fluid and had watched the scraper while moving it.

At 10:33, Feld absorbed the remaining fluid.

The defective fitting was replaced, the spill area was cleaned, and the assembly passed a standard maintenance inspection before testing resumed at 14:08.

At 14:19, a retaining ring in the reflected pulley assembly fractured during a lift at 61% of rated capacity. The suspended test mass fell 1.3 m onto the laboratory floor.

No personnel were injured.

Examination found that the retaining ring had failed through an ordinary fracture originating at a shallow surface defect.

Feld had never been briefed on {{SCP_ID}} and had not participated in activation testing.

Subsequent matched-cohort trials reproduced Feld’s actions with equivalent assemblies and produced the same failure distribution as intentional activations.

Review of recorded Foundation equipment failures identified four additional probable accidental activations occurring between 1998 and 2023. The least consequential involved a laboratory cabinet hinge. The most serious caused the collapse of an unmanned construction hoist during installation at Site-73.

{{SCP_ID}} was reclassified from Euclid to Keter following confirmation that the minimum procedure could occur unintentionally during ordinary maintenance activity.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-4: Civilian Review

The Foundation initiated a retrospective review of civilian mechanical failures for which sufficient pre-incident visual evidence could be obtained.

A case could be classified as a probable accidental activation only when analysts could establish:

  • the presence of a continuous reflective liquid surface;
  • three crossings by a rigid metallic object;
  • removal of the surface within 120 seconds;
  • a probable direct view of the reflection by the operator; and
  • a later mechanically plausible failure within the reflected system or region.

The first review identified seven probable activations among sixty-seven incidents with adequate pre-failure coverage.

All seven had previously received conventional mechanical explanations.

Case {{SCP_NUMBER}}-04

Date: 1997-09-12
System: Freight-loading gantry
Outcome: Property damage; two minor injuries

A warehouse loading gantry collapsed following fracture of a heavily corroded anchor bolt.

Video recorded approximately eleven hours earlier shows a maintenance worker removing rainwater from the loading apron. The gantry is reflected in a shallow pool beside the worker.

While repositioning equipment, the worker moves the steel nozzle of a wet vacuum through the reflected gantry three times. The pool is then drained.

The anchor bolt was sufficiently degraded to explain the collapse without anomalous involvement.

Classification: Probable activation.

Case {{SCP_NUMBER}}-11

Date: 2011-04-03
System: Mobile construction crane
Outcome: One fatality; three injuries

A crane boom collapsed following failure of a luffing-cable termination.

A sequence of photographs taken during morning setup shows the crane reflected in a shallow wash tray while two workers clean concrete residue with a metal-handled tool. Reconstruction of the workers’ positions and the tool’s successive locations indicates three complete crossings from the probable viewpoint of one worker. The tray was then emptied into a waste container.

The cable failure was consistent with progressive damage already present at the termination.

Classification: Probable activation.

No physical characteristic distinguishes either failure from an equivalent non-anomalous accident.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-5: Interstate 35W

During expansion of the civilian review, analysts examined the August 1, 2007 collapse of the Interstate 35W bridge over the Mississippi River in Minneapolis, Minnesota.

The collapse killed thirteen people and injured 145.

The National Transportation Safety Board attributed the collapse to inadequate load capacity in gusset plates at the U10 nodes resulting from a design error, together with increased bridge weight, traffic, and concentrated construction loads present on the day of the collapse.

Project engineers reviewed the published investigation and independently agreed that these conditions were sufficient to explain the failure.

No anomalous material property has been identified in recovered bridge components.

Foundation interest resulted from six unpublished digital photographs taken by a subcontractor during an inspection beneath the bridge earlier that afternoon.

The photographs show a worker kneeling on a temporary access platform while rinsing a steel measuring rod in a shallow plastic tray.

From the worker’s reconstructed head position, a portion of the deck truss was reflected in the water.

Across a sequence lasting approximately seventy seconds, the measuring rod passes completely through the reflected truss three times.

The final image shows the worker emptying the tray.

The surviving photographs do not establish whether the worker was looking at the reflection during all three crossings.

No evidence indicates that the worker possessed knowledge of {{SCP_ID}}.

The suspected activation occurred approximately four hours before the collapse.

The bridge already possessed a documented and independently sufficient mechanism for catastrophic structural failure. Project Engineering therefore rejected a proposal to identify {{SCP_ID}} as the cause of the collapse.

The incident is recorded as:

Probable accidental activation — confidence moderate.

The review finding concludes:

{{SCP_ID}} provides no basis to dispute the established cause of the collapse.

The U10 gusset plates possessed inadequate load capacity. The loading conditions present on August 1, 2007 were real. The collapse sequence is mechanically explicable.

The surviving evidence cannot answer the narrower question:

Would the same failure have occurred at 18:05 without the suspected activation?

Following this review, screening criteria were expanded to include major transportation and infrastructure failures.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-6: Current Assessment

As of the most recent review, twenty-three civilian incidents have been classified as probable accidental {{SCP_ID}} activations.

Five resulted in one or more fatalities.

These cases should not be interpreted as an estimate of {{SCP_ID}}’s contribution to mechanical failures generally.

Confirmation depends on evidence recorded before the failure, usually from a useful visual angle. Such evidence does not exist for the overwhelming majority of historical accidents.

An {{SCP_ID}}-induced failure is also physically indistinguishable from a non-anomalous failure produced under the same conditions.

The total historical incidence is therefore considered unknowable.

No confirmed evidence indicates deliberate modern use of {{SCP_ID}} outside Foundation testing. Current containment efforts remain divided between suppressing intentional reconstruction and reducing the likelihood of accidental activation around critical systems.

Historical Targeting Review

Foundation testing has not demonstrated reliable component-level targeting.

Surviving ███████ texts frequently claim otherwise.

Project historians attribute these claims to selective preservation, retrospective interpretation, translation uncertainty, and the tendency of military and religious records to document apparent successes while omitting failures.

Three archaeological findings remain disputed.

A siege tablet recovered from ██████ specifies failure of what has been translated as “the lower bronze tooth of the western gate.” Excavation identified an ancient repair to the gate’s lower western hinge assembly.

A naval account dated to approximately 1120 BCE describes a rite directed at “the collar beneath the forward mast.” A wreck tentatively associated with the engagement shows replacement of the corresponding mast fitting during antiquity.

In 2025, a fragment previously translated as referring generally to a quarry lifting frame was reinterpreted after the device was reconstructed from surviving postholes and ironwork. The inscription appears to identify a retaining component on the frame’s eastern side.

The only recovered component exhibiting catastrophic ancient damage corresponds to that position.

None of these findings demonstrates that historical practitioners could select an individual component. Each remains compatible with coincidence, incomplete evidence, or interpretation influenced by prior knowledge of {{SCP_ID}}.

During a 2025 multispectral reexamination, conservators reconstructed a damaged instruction on the original ██████ gate tablet that had not been included in the working translation used during the Foundation’s reduction trials:

After the third crossing, keep the bronze point upon the lower tooth until the blood is still.

The post-crossing hold is not included in current activation procedures. Its omission has no measurable effect on activation reliability.
