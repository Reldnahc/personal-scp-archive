Special Containment Procedures

{{SCP_ID}} is maintained in the mechanical workshop at Site-51 and stored in a locked cabinet when not in use. Technical personnel may consult {{SCP_ID}} during equipment maintenance and repair. Work performed on containment-critical systems following consultation with {{SCP_ID}} must be functionally verified by a second technician before the system is returned to service.

Use of {{SCP_ID}} during practical certification examinations or scheduled unaided-diagnostics exercises is prohibited. Removal from Site-51 for longer than twenty-four hours requires Site Director approval.

Description

{{SCP_ID}} is a handheld electronic diagnostic instrument equipped with two wired probes and a monochrome liquid-crystal display. It bears no manufacturer name, model number, or serial number. Examination of its internal components has not identified the source of its anomalous function.

When both probes are placed in contact with a malfunctioning mechanical or electrical system, {{SCP_ID}} displays a brief description of the fault and the corrective action required. The probes do not need to contact an electrical circuit or diagnostic interface; placement against an exterior housing is generally sufficient.

If several faults are present, {{SCP_ID}} identifies only the fault currently preventing normal operation. After the indicated repair is completed, the device may be applied again to identify the next fault. {{SCP_ID}} does not provide diagrams, explain the basis of its diagnosis, or describe how to perform the repair.

{{SCP_ID}} functions on equipment lacking any compatible diagnostic interface, including obsolete machinery and several anomalous systems. It has not demonstrated an ability to identify the source or mechanism of anomalous behavior, but will identify conventional defects affecting anomalous equipment.

Since its acquisition in 2012, {{SCP_ID}} has been used in 8,614 documented repairs. No incorrect diagnosis has been confirmed. On fourteen occasions, repairs could not be completed because the indicated component was unavailable or inaccessible.

{{SCP_ID}} can be applied to itself by placing both probes against its casing. Failures of its display backlight and battery-charging circuit have been diagnosed and repaired in this manner using commercially available components.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-1

In 2023, {{SCP_ID}} was transferred to Site-17 for thirty-one days to evaluate its performance on anomalous machinery. The evaluation confirmed that {{SCP_ID}} could identify conventional mechanical and electrical defects in twelve anomalous devices but produced no useful response concerning their anomalous properties.

During its absence, the median technician labor recorded per completed equipment repair at Site-51 increased from 3.4 hours to 11.7 hours. At the end of the evaluation period, 186 maintenance requests were awaiting diagnosis. During the preceding twelve months, the mean open diagnostic backlog recorded at weekly maintenance audits had been twenty-three.

{{SCP_ID}} was returned to Site-51 on 2023-11-02. Median technician labor per completed repair returned to its pre-transfer level within two days.

A review initiated during the evaluation was completed following the device’s return. Fifty-two technicians were asked to diagnose six common faults introduced into standard mechanical and electrical training rigs without anomalous assistance. A passing score required the correct isolation of at least five faults within the time limits used in the final pre-2015 practical certification examination.

The Site-51 workshop curriculum was revised in 2015 to formally incorporate device-assisted diagnosis. For the purposes of the review, routine use was defined as consultation of {{SCP_ID}} in more than half of a technician’s repair assignments during the preceding twelve months. All other use was classified as infrequent. No technician certified under the revised curriculum met the criteria for infrequent use.

Certification requirements and total instructional hours had not been reduced following the introduction of {{SCP_ID}}. Review of archived course material found that eighteen hours of the 2012 workshop curriculum had been devoted to fault isolation and diagnostic procedure. By 2022, four hours remained. The removed material had been replaced by instruction in component replacement, repair verification, and interpretation of {{SCP_ID}} output.

Maintenance records showed a corresponding change in practice. In 2013, 82% of work orders documented an independently determined suspected cause before consultation of {{SCP_ID}}. By 2022, this figure had fallen to 18%. In 71% of the 2022 work orders reviewed, the only diagnostic information recorded was a transcription of the device’s display.

A mandatory unaided-diagnostics program was introduced following the review. After eight weeks, scheduled instruction was reduced from four hours per technician per week to one hour per month because personnel assigned to training were unavailable to address the maintenance backlog.

A separate proposal requiring technicians to document an independent preliminary diagnosis before consulting {{SCP_ID}} was rejected. The review committee determined that the requirement would increase repair completion times without producing an immediate improvement in repair accuracy.

No further off-site evaluations have been authorized.
