Special Containment Procedures

{{SCP_ID}} is stored in a climate-controlled document case at the Site-19 Records Annex. Removal requires authorization from the Records Director.

{{SCP_ID}} is not to be used in the preparation of operational documentation.

Existing documents produced with {{SCP_ID}} require no special storage beyond normal archival controls.

Description

{{SCP_ID}} is a black fountain pen manufactured by the █████ Pen Company in approximately 1948. It accepts conventional ink and contains no unusual physical components.

Text handwritten with {{SCP_ID}} is understood more quickly and accurately than equivalent text produced by conventional means. Readers show improved recall, fewer procedural errors, and unusually consistent interpretations of ambiguous or poorly written passages.

The effect does not correct factual errors or add information absent from the text.

Photographs, photocopies, microfilm, and scanned images of affected writing retain the anomaly. Retyped or manually transcribed versions do not. Text added with another writing instrument is unaffected.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-1: Discovery

{{SCP_ID}} was identified at Site-19 in 1952.

On 04/17/1952, records clerk Marian Hale used her personal fountain pen to prepare a temporary replacement for a damaged maintenance stencil. The document was rejected by Senior Technical Editor ███████ due to Hale's handwriting, which was cramped and contained several malformed characters and nonstandard abbreviations.

Before ordering a new copy, ███████ circulated the page among six members of the records staff and asked them to mark any words they could not read.

All six produced identical transcriptions.

When questioned individually, all six also gave the same expansions for three abbreviations Hale had not previously used in Foundation documentation.

Hale confirmed that these interpretations matched her intent.

A typewritten copy containing the same wording was prepared. Reviewers disagreed on the meaning of two of the abbreviations and required significantly more time to answer questions concerning the procedure.

Hale was asked to reproduce the same paragraph using five writing instruments available in the office.

The anomalous effect occurred only with {{SCP_ID}}.

The object was transferred to the Anomalous Materials Division for testing.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-2: Testing

In the largest controlled trial, sixty Foundation maintenance personnel were divided into two groups and instructed to service an unfamiliar pump assembly using the same written procedure.

Group A received photographic copies of a master handwritten with {{SCP_ID}}. Group B received a typewritten transcription with identical wording and layout.

Group A completed the procedure an average of 21% faster and committed nine total procedural errors. Group B committed twenty-seven.

Participants were instructed to request clarification whenever they considered an instruction unclear. Group A submitted two clarification requests. Group B submitted nineteen.

A second trial reproduced these results using deliberately awkward but technically correct instructions. Researchers noted that subjects using affected documents frequently described the material as poorly written while nevertheless interpreting it consistently.

The final testing report recommended {{SCP_ID}} for controlled document preparation.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-3: Foundation Use

{{SCP_ID}} was assigned to the Central Documentation Office in 1956.

After a procedure completed normal technical and administrative review, a records clerk copied the approved text by hand using {{SCP_ID}}. Photographic masters were then used to produce affected copies for distribution.

The process was used primarily for maintenance procedures, emergency instructions, and selected containment documentation. By 1974, approximately 11% of active Foundation procedures had an {{SCP_ID}} master.

Affected documents were associated with lower procedural-error rates and substantially fewer requests for clarification.

Use declined during the 1980s as Foundation documentation became longer and more frequently revised. Because changes required preparation of an entirely new handwritten master, {{SCP_ID}} became increasingly incompatible with electronic document production.

Routine use ended on 03/18/1991.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-4: Archival Review

In 1994, Procedure 41-B was converted into the Foundation Electronic Documentation System.

Procedure 41-B governed maintenance of the primary coolant system for SCP-████. Its {{SCP_ID}} master had been prepared in 1972 and remained in circulation through a series of appended revisions.

During conversion, Technician Laura Mendes identified an error in Step 14. The procedure directed personnel to close isolation valve V-4. Engineering diagrams and equipment markings identified the required valve as V-5. Closing V-4 would isolate the emergency return line instead.

Review of archived copies found handwritten corrections on twenty-six documents. The earliest was dated seventeen days after Procedure 41-B entered circulation.

Typical annotations read:

V-4 IN TEXT = V-5 ON PANEL

and:

NUMBER WRONG. USE WEST VALVE.

Procedure 41-B underwent forty-one scheduled reviews between 1972 and 1994. Review signatures included twelve mechanical engineers, nineteen certified maintenance technicians, and four containment specialists.

No amendment request concerning Step 14 was filed during this period.

The {{SCP_ID}} master was transcribed into ordinary electronic text on 06/18/1994.

Mendes submitted the first correction request on 06/19/1994.
