Special Containment Procedures

All confirmed instances of {{SCP_ID}} are to be stored in individual archival enclosures at the Site-41 Historical Anomalies Archive. Digital scans, transcriptions, and bibliographies are to be maintained on standalone archival systems with no connection to external search, library, or archival services. No confirmed instance or complete bibliography is to be catalogued in a publicly accessible database.

Personnel handling {{SCP_ID}} may examine, photograph, transcribe, or analyze its text and physical construction. Personnel are not to attempt to locate, request, reconstruct, establish the provenance of, contact any person or institution associated with, or otherwise verify any source cited by an instance unless specifically authorized by the project lead for containment purposes.

This restriction applies regardless of whether the cited source appears fictitious, malformed, impossible, future-dated, or already documented.

Unauthorized search-engine queries, library requests, correspondence, database lookups, archival searches, automated retrieval attempts, and similar activity involving a citation from {{SCP_ID}} are to be logged and terminated immediately.

Materials believed to have manifested as a result of {{SCP_ID}} are to be isolated and physically examined before further research is permitted. Most such materials exhibit no continuing anomalous properties. Materials that initially test negative are to remain isolated and undergo periodic compositional reanalysis. Materials exhibiting the physical characteristics described below are to be redesignated as new instances of {{SCP_ID}}.

Foundation personnel embedded within major library systems, universities, archives, estate-clearance companies, antiquarian booksellers, and large-scale donation processors are to report bibliographic discrepancies consistent with {{SCP_ID}} activity. These include publications containing clearly anachronistic or future-dated references, sources appearing in current catalogues but absent from preserved records, and materials recovered following previously unsuccessful retrieval attempts.

Suspected instances are to be acquired through routine channels and forwarded for compositional analysis. Embedded personnel are not to investigate suspicious citations beyond what is necessary to identify and secure the publication.

Authorization is not to be granted for active attempts to verify future-dated citations.

Description

{{SCP_ID}} is a designation applied to a collection of otherwise unrelated nonfiction books exhibiting a shared textual anomaly and a shared material anomaly.

Known instances differ in title, author, subject, publisher, stated date of publication, writing style, dimensions, binding, and apparent country of origin. No recurring title, symbol, phrase, or publisher has been identified.

Reliable provenance for the instances themselves is generally incomplete. No confirmed instance has been traced through an uninterrupted chain of custody extending to its stated date of publication.

Subjects represented among recovered instances include municipal drainage, nineteenth-century botany, regional military history, industrial ceramics, legal procedure, agricultural economics, and the history of public broadcasting.

Each instance is internally credible within its stated subject. Most factual claims are accurate, and most citations refer to sources present in preserved records predating recovery of the instance.

A minority of citations, however, refer to sources for which no evidence of existence can initially be found.

Attempts to verify such a source may result in the source becoming extant.

This effect is designated {{SCP_ID}}-1.

No individual investigative action has been demonstrated to be either necessary or sufficient to trigger {{SCP_ID}}-1.

Manifestation has followed:

  • repeated library catalogue searches;
  • archival retrieval requests;
  • interlibrary loan requests;
  • correspondence with alleged publishers or authors;
  • direct examination of physical collections;
  • database and newspaper-index searches;
  • requests delegated to uninformed third parties; and
  • combinations of the above.

A single casual search has not produced a confirmed manifestation. Sustained investigation appears to increase the probability of {{SCP_ID}}-1, but no minimum duration, number of actions, number of investigators, or degree of intent has been established.

Manifestation is not necessarily immediate. In controlled trials, cited sources have become extant between eleven minutes and nine days after all verification activity ceased.

Once manifested, the source is accompanied by whatever physical, documentary, and historical context is necessary for it to plausibly exist.

This may include aged physical copies, catalogue records, accession documents, publication records, correspondence, institutional files, references in unrelated publications, and human memories.

These changes do not consistently overwrite evidence collected before manifestation.

A Foundation screenshot showing zero search results may remain unchanged while the same database later lists the manifested source as having been catalogued decades earlier. Video may show an empty archival shelf before a document manifests there. Laboratory records created before manifestation may continue to identify a genetic profile as unmatched after the corresponding individual acquires an apparently complete historical existence.

Human memory is affected inconsistently. Some individuals acquire memories consistent with the newly established provenance. Others retain the previous state of reality. Some report both.

{{SCP_ID}}-1 therefore does not appear to produce a complete retroactive revision of history. It instead establishes a present state consistent with a newly imposed past while inconsistently preserving evidence of the preceding state.

The scope of alteration varies.

When a cited source can plausibly exist without substantial external events, the apparent alteration may be limited to the source and its immediate publication history.

When a source requires an underlying event, institution, individual, or sequence of events in order to possess the claimed provenance, reality is altered to provide them.

A manifested accident report may therefore require the accident it describes.

A court transcript may require the hearing.

A biography may require the person.

The Foundation has not identified a reliable method of determining the required scope of an {{SCP_ID}}-1 event before manifestation occurs.

Most manifested sources are non-anomalous once established.

In rare cases, a manifested source later meets the criteria for designation as {{SCP_ID}} and contains further citations capable of producing {{SCP_ID}}-1 events. No feature of the originating citation predicts whether this will occur.

Physical Composition

The pages of every confirmed {{SCP_ID}} instance are visually and mechanically consistent with ordinary commercial paper appropriate to the stated date and place of publication.

The sheets are cellulose-based and contain no intact human cells or tissue.

Chemical analysis instead shows ordinary cellulose fibers interspersed with denatured human proteins and fragmented nuclear DNA at concentrations incompatible with accidental contamination.

Each instance contains material derived from multiple genetically distinct human contributors.

Most profiles cannot be associated with any known individual, living or deceased. Several have subsequently been matched to individuals absent from records preserved before related manifestations.

The method by which this material was incorporated without detectably altering the paper's texture, strength, or appearance has not been identified.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-1: Discovery

The first confirmed instance of {{SCP_ID}} was recovered in 2023 from the reference collection of the ███████ County Library in Wisconsin.

The volume was titled Flood Control and Municipal Expansion in the Western Great Lakes, 1946–1982, attributed to historian Philip R. Amsden and allegedly published by North Country Academic Press in 1987.

No corresponding acquisition record could be located, and no staff member could establish when the volume had entered the collection.

Apart from its bibliography, its contents were unremarkable.

A graduate student using the book for research identified a citation to:

Larkin, Harold V. Preliminary Drainage Assessment: Lower ███████ Basin. County Engineering Office, 1963.

No such report could be located.

The student submitted two catalogue requests, contacted the county engineering office, searched municipal meeting records, and later visited the county archive in person.

Archive staff reported that no report matching the citation existed.

Six days later, an archivist contacted the student to state that the requested document had been located in a mislabeled records box.

The report was dated 1963.

Its paper, ink, typewriter characteristics, municipal stamps, and signatures were consistent with the period.

The county archive catalogue now listed the report as accessioned in 1971.

The archivist responsible for locating it stated that she remembered seeing the report several years earlier.

The graduate student did not.

A Foundation web crawler flagged the case when the current county catalogue began to disagree with cached captures made before the student's requests. Those captures did not list the report.

The physical box containing the report displayed an accession number sequentially consistent with records received in 1971. No alteration to the surrounding files was detected.

The recovered book was transferred to Site-41.

Laboratory analysis identified human biological material within its paper stock.

Forty-three distinct genetic profiles were isolated. Forty-two could not be associated with a documented individual.

Profile 17 was consistent with DNA recovered from a preserved shaving kit attributed to Harold V. Larkin. Current records indicate that Larkin lived from 1928 to 1999 and worked for ███████ County between 1956 and 1974.

Foundation database captures made before manifestation of the report contain no record of Larkin.

No relationship between Larkin and the manufacture of the book has been established.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-2: Controlled Verification

Initial experiments were conducted to determine whether the effect extended beyond government records.

Citation 31 in the first instance referred to a 1976 journal article titled Seasonal Frost Effects in Shallow Municipal Culverts by E. Marwick and J. L. Tolsen.

No article matching the citation could be located.

Over five days, researchers searched academic databases and university special collections, submitted a library request, and contacted the publishing society named in the citation. No record of the article was found, and the inquiry was returned as undeliverable.

During this period, a retired civil-engineering professor was interviewed:

Researcher: Have you ever encountered Marwick or Tolsen in the frost-heave literature?

Professor █████: No. Those names don't mean anything to me.

The experiment was terminated.

Two days later, a Foundation researcher unrelated to the test discovered a reference to the article while reviewing an independently documented 1981 engineering bibliography.

Further examination established that the article now existed.

Three university libraries held physical copies of the relevant journal volume. All three displayed acquisition markings predating Foundation testing.

When recontacted, Professor █████ stated that she had used the article while teaching in 1984. She did not remember the recorded interview.

The manifested article was physically non-anomalous. Every source in its bibliography was present in Foundation records predating the test.

Following six additional controlled manifestations, Dr. Mara Desai recommended suspension of all verification not required for immediate containment.

Her memorandum is reproduced in part below.

We are continuing to describe these events as if documents are appearing.

That is not what we have observed.

A document cannot have been accessioned in 1971 unless somebody accessioned it. A paper cannot cite a conference unless there was a conference. A dead engineer cannot have a shaving kit unless there was an engineer.

Every time we verify one of these citations, we are not retrieving information. We are accepting whatever amount of history is required to make the citation legitimate.

We do not know the size of that history before we request it.

I recommend that all verification not required for immediate containment cease.

The recommendation was rejected.

Continued experimental testing was approved on the basis that all alterations observed to that point had been limited in scope and that further characterization was considered necessary for containment.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-3: Utilization Trial 4

Following repeated successful manifestations, project administration authorized limited investigation of citations believed likely to produce information of practical value.

Utilization Trial 4 concerned a citation appearing in {{SCP_ID}}-3, a 2016 monograph on high-temperature industrial ceramics.

The citation referred to:

Venn, T., Okafor, B., and Leung, S. “Low-Cycle Failure Suppression in Composite Bearing Sleeves.” Proceedings of the North American Materials Reliability Consortium, Vol. 12, 2009.

No organization called the North American Materials Reliability Consortium could be identified.

The cited paper was selected because its title suggested relevance to persistent bearing failures in Foundation remote-handling equipment.

Among the available candidates, it appeared to have a comparatively limited dependency footprint: it implicated no disaster, public works project, government action, or historically prominent individual and appeared to require only an obscure professional organization and its associated researchers.

Dr. Desai objected to the test.

The objection was overruled.

Verification began on 08/11/2025.

Researchers searched academic indexes, conference registries, patent databases, archived professional-society websites, and university records. Three inquiries were sent to institutions associated with researchers sharing the cited names.

No source was located.

All verification activity ceased at 17:40.

At 09:12 the following morning, the North American Materials Reliability Consortium existed.

Foundation comparison with preserved records identified the following changes:

  • The organization had been founded in 1988 and maintained an administrative office in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, with laboratory facilities in Illinois and Ontario.
  • It had conducted twenty-one conferences between 1989 and 2025, and its proceedings were held by more than forty university libraries.
  • At least 312 individuals possessed current or former employment records associated with the organization, and eleven patents cited consortium-funded research.
  • Two industrial buildings occupying land previously documented as warehouse space were now registered to consortium affiliates and exhibited construction consistent with the early 1990s.
  • A materials-science professor at Northwestern University now possessed a twenty-seven-year publication history involving the consortium. Foundation records collected the previous day showed no such affiliation.

The 2009 paper existed.

Its proposed bearing treatment was functional.

The process reduced failure rates in subsequent Foundation testing by 18%.

Collateral changes were not limited to the consortium.

Foundation comparison teams identified altered employment histories, patent ownership, grant allocation, university hiring, and commercial partnerships affecting more than sixty unrelated organizations.

The total number of individuals whose present circumstances differed from preserved pre-manifestation records could not be determined.

No mass-casualty event or other immediate disaster was associated with the manifestation.

Dr. Desai's original moratorium proposal was adopted without modification eleven hours after the initial scope of the alterations was confirmed.

No further experimental or utilization trials have been authorized.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-4: Secondary Instances

On 10/03/2025, a technical monograph previously manifested from a citation in {{SCP_ID}}-3 was found to contain human-derived material within its paper stock.

Initial compositional analysis performed immediately after its recovery had detected no human-derived material. The original analytical report remains unchanged.

Comparison with the initial digital scan found no alteration to the text or bibliography. No Foundation record identifies when the composition of the paper changed.

The monograph was redesignated {{SCP_ID}}-7.

{{SCP_ID}}-7 contained 214 citations.

Two referred to sources for which no evidence of existence could be located.

Neither has been investigated.

Most manifested sources examined to date have not undergone a comparable change.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-5: Future Citation

{{SCP_ID}}-9 was recovered in February 2026 from a box of donated books awaiting processing at a church-run resale store in Iowa.

It is a 1974 history of municipal rail development in the upper Midwest.

No previous owner could be identified from the volume or accompanying donation records.

Of its 391 citations, 388 refer to legitimate sources.

Two refer to sources that cannot presently be located.

The remaining citation is:

Federal Commission on the Lake Hart Tunnel Disaster. Final Technical Report and Recommendations. Washington, D.C., 2034.

At the time of recovery, no project using that name appeared in Foundation transportation records or public datasets archived before the book was examined.

No active attempt to verify the citation was authorized.

On 02/19/2026, Research Assistant Colin Marsh entered the citation title into a public search engine from a non-Foundation workstation.

Marsh reported that he intended only to confirm that the citation returned no results and did not consider this an attempt at verification.

No results were found.

No further searches were performed.

On 03/02/2026, an automated Foundation system monitoring retroactive changes to public records identified the Lake Hart Regional Transit Tunnel, a proposed project connecting ███████ and ███████ beneath Lake Hart.

Current records indicate that preliminary feasibility work began in 2024.

Foundation archives captured in January 2026 contain no reference to the project.

Public planning documents now attribute the proposal to a regional transportation initiative established in 2023.

Several officials involved state that they have worked on the project for more than two years.

Others deny having heard of it before March.

No document titled Final Technical Report and Recommendations exists.

No Federal Commission on the Lake Hart Tunnel Disaster exists.

Construction of the Lake Hart Regional Transit Tunnel has not begun.

It is unknown whether Marsh's search initiated an incomplete manifestation, whether future-dated citations generate prerequisite history independently, or whether the project is unrelated.

Dr. Desai requested permanent suppression of the project through conventional political and financial means.

The request was denied on the grounds that Foundation interference could itself contribute to circumstances necessary for the cited report to exist.

No further action has been authorized.
