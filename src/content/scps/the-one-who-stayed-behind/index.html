Special Containment Procedures

All surface entrances to {{SCP_ID}} have been sealed with reinforced concrete except Shaft 2, which is maintained for remote inspection.

Shaft 2 is secured by two independently locked steel gates. Access below the 410 m level is prohibited. No human being is to pass Survey Marker {{SCP_NUMBER}}-A.

Unmanned exploration is permitted.

The telephone located in the Shaft 2 hoist room is to remain connected to its original underground line. Calls originating from {{SCP_ID}} are to be automatically recorded. Personnel are not to answer.

Under no circumstances are rescue operations to be initiated in response to telephone calls, radio transmissions, percussion signals, written messages, or other evidence suggesting that Foundation personnel remain within {{SCP_ID}} after an authorized team has returned to the surface.

Requests by personnel to verify their own exit from {{SCP_ID}} are to be referred for psychological evaluation.

Description

{{SCP_ID}} is the former ███████ No. 4 coal mine near █████████, West Virginia. Mining operations ceased in 1962.

The anomaly manifests following human entry into sections of the mine below approximately 430 m. It most commonly begins after one or more entrants return to the surface, when the mine's internal telephone system may receive a call originating from below the abandoned workings.

Callers usually identify themselves as individuals who have just exited the mine. In these cases, the corresponding individual is present on the surface. Calls attributed to personnel who entered {{SCP_ID}} in response to an earlier call and failed to return have also been recorded; no surface counterpart was present in those incidents.

Callers possess the voice, memories, personal knowledge, and mannerisms of the individual they claim to be. They accurately describe events occurring during the preceding expedition, including events not recorded electronically or witnessed by other personnel. Callers generally state that the remainder of the team left them behind. They frequently report darkness, equipment failure, injury, or inability to locate the route to the surface.

Callers for whom a corresponding surface individual exists have never acknowledged that individual's existence. When informed that the person whose identity they claim has already exited {{SCP_ID}}, they have consistently accused Foundation personnel of error or deception.

The underground telephone network is physically disconnected in multiple locations. Calls continue to occur.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-1: Discovery

Foundation attention was drawn to {{SCP_ID}} following an incident on 09/17/1998 involving four amateur explorers.

At approximately 18:20, all four individuals exited the mine through an unsecured ventilation adit. At 18:43, the caretaker of a nearby equipment yard received a call through a telephone line formerly belonging to ███████ Coal Company.

The caller identified himself as Matthew Keene, one of the four explorers. Keene stated that he had become separated from the others and was unable to locate the ventilation adit.

The caretaker contacted emergency services. Keene was standing beside the caretaker when the call was made.

The caretaker initially believed the call to be a prank. Keene was permitted to speak with the caller. The following portion was reconstructed from emergency-dispatch recordings.

KEENE: Who is this?

CALLER: Matt.

KEENE: No. Who is this?

CALLER: What the fuck are you doing?

KEENE: Where are you?

CALLER: I don't know. Past the pump room. I came back to the junction and you were gone.

KEENE: I'm outside.

[Silence.]

CALLER: No you're not.

KEENE: I'm standing outside.

CALLER: Is Derek there?

KEENE: Yeah.

CALLER: Ask him what he found in the lunchbox.

[Background conversation.]

KEENE: He says nothing.

CALLER: He's lying.

KEENE: What was in it?

CALLER: That picture of the woman with the red hair. He put it in his jacket because he didn't want Laura to see it.

[23 seconds silence.]

CALLER: Matt?

KEENE: How do you know that?

CALLER: Because I was standing next to him.

KEENE: So was I.

[Silence.]

CALLER: Then come get me.

Explorer Derek ███████ subsequently admitted removing a photograph from an abandoned miner's lunchbox. Keene stated that he had witnessed this and had agreed not to tell the fourth member of the group, ███████'s girlfriend.

A county rescue team entered the mine at 21:06. Three rescuers disappeared.

Calls subsequently received through the same telephone line identified all three missing rescuers.

The operation was abandoned after thirty-six hours. Foundation personnel assumed control of the site.

No remains were recovered.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-2: Exploration 1

On 05/11/2000, a four-person Foundation team entered {{SCP_ID}}. Personnel were equipped with body cameras, redundant radios, inertial navigation systems, and continuous biometric monitoring.

The team consisted of Agent Michael Mallory, Agent ███████, Dr. Nolan Pike, and D-5510.

Personnel reached a depth of approximately 510 m before returning. All four individuals remained within visual range of at least one other team member throughout the expedition.

All four exited {{SCP_ID}} at 13:52.

At 14:07, the hoist-room telephone rang. The call was answered by Dr. Pike before recording protocols had been established.

PIKE: Site team. Identify yourself.

CALLER: Mallory.

Agent Mallory, present in the hoist room, turns toward Dr. Pike.

PIKE: Repeat.

CALLER: Mike Mallory. My lamp's gone. I think I'm back near that flooded section.

PIKE: Agent Mallory is standing beside me.

[8 seconds silence.]

CALLER: Nolan, don't do this.

PIKE: What is your current position?

CALLER: I just told you I don't know.

PIKE: What happened to the rest of the team?

CALLER: You left.

PIKE: Describe the point where you became separated.

CALLER: After the ladder.

PIKE: There was no ladder.

CALLER: The wooden one.

PIKE: We didn't encounter a ladder.

CALLER: Yes we did.

Agent Mallory requests the receiver. Dr. Pike complies.

MALLORY: Hey.

[5 seconds silence.]

CALLER: What the fuck.

MALLORY: Yeah.

CALLER: Who is that?

MALLORY: Mike Mallory.

CALLER: Nolan.

MALLORY: He's right here.

CALLER: Put Nolan back on.

MALLORY: Before we went down, what did I tell you in the truck?

CALLER: Fuck you.

MALLORY: Answer it.

[12 seconds silence.]

CALLER: You said if the D-boy fell, we weren't carrying him back up.

MALLORY: Anything else?

CALLER: You said ███████ was going to get somebody killed because he keeps clipping his light to his right shoulder.

Agent Mallory discontinued the call.

The conversation continued for nineteen minutes after Mallory left the hoist room. The caller repeatedly requested that personnel return underground.

At 14:31, the caller stated that he could hear movement elsewhere in the tunnel. At 14:34, he ceased responding.

The line remained open for another six minutes. No further sounds were recorded.

Agent Mallory denied encountering a wooden ladder during the expedition. Body-camera footage confirmed his account.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-3: Survey Anomaly

Remote vehicles were deployed to locate the positions described during calls.

Several callers described passages not present on mine plans. Three such passages have subsequently been located. None existed during previous remote surveys.

Excavation records indicate that mining operations never reached these areas. The newly identified workings differ significantly from the original mine. Timber supports are present but bear no manufacturer stamps. Tool marks are consistent with manual excavation. Coal seams exposed in these passages have not been commercially worked.

Low-frequency analysis of seven recorded calls identified a repeating mechanical pulse beneath the callers' voices. Two mine-equipment consultants identified the pulse as consistent with a compressed-air haulage engine removed from No. 4 in 1952. A third attributed it to degradation in the surface recording apparatus. The source remains undetermined.

In 2003, Remote Vehicle {{SCP_NUMBER}}-4 located a wooden ladder approximately 610 m beyond Survey Marker {{SCP_NUMBER}}-A. The ladder was lying against the western wall of an unmapped passage.

Agent Mallory identified it from photographs as:

“Probably the ladder the other one was talking about.”

The ladder was recovered robotically.

A partial latent fingerprint was found on the fourth rung.

It matched Agent Mallory's right thumb.

Mallory denied having touched the object.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-4: Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-12

Further manned testing was authorized in 2004.

D-1184 was selected as the sole human subject. D-1184 was equipped with two independently monitored body cameras, three location transmitters, biometric telemetry, and a harness attached to 600 m of high-tensile retrieval cable.

The subject was instructed to pass Survey Marker {{SCP_NUMBER}}-A, walk 50 m, and return.

D-1184 crossed the marker at 09:11. He reached the turnaround point at 09:17. He returned across Survey Marker {{SCP_NUMBER}}-A at 09:23 and reached the surface at 09:41.

No loss of telemetry occurred.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-5: Recovered Material

Remote exploration continued.

At 11:44 on 06/02/2004, a crawler located a length of braided retrieval cable approximately 730 m beyond Survey Marker {{SCP_NUMBER}}-A. The cable terminated at a damaged climbing harness.

Both matched equipment issued to D-1184.

The original harness and cable remained in Foundation storage.

Blood was present on the recovered harness. DNA profiling matched D-1184.

A second crawler subsequently located one Foundation-issue boot, three empty emergency-water pouches, a damaged body camera, a human fingernail, and approximately 4 m of disturbed coal and shale consistent with attempted hand excavation.

The body camera bore the same serial number as the unit issued to D-1184, which remained functional in Foundation storage with its original memory intact.

No further manned exploration was authorized.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-6: Historical Records

In 2009, Foundation researchers obtained payroll, medical, and accident records from the former ███████ Coal Company.

Between 1934 and closure of the mine in 1962, nineteen incidents occurred in which a worker was reported missing underground.

In eight cases, the supposedly missing worker subsequently appears in payroll records. Six continued employment at the mine. Two moved to other states. One, ███████ █████, lived until 1991.

Despite this, company rescue logs document extended searches for each individual.

The following entry appears in a shift supervisor's journal dated 08/03/1947:

Bobby came out with the rest at 4:10.

Phone rang at 4:26.

Bobby called from 7 West saying the roof had come down and Sam left him.

Bobby heard him.

Wouldn't let us answer again.

Sam and Turner went back down anyway.

The next entry states:

Found Sam.

Did not find Turner.

Bobby quit.

Company records show Robert “Bobby” █████ remained employed until 1958.

No record of a second Robert █████ exists.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-7: Borehole 3

In 2022, geological imaging identified a large void approximately 80 m beneath the deepest documented portion of {{SCP_ID}}.

The void could not be reached through known workings.

A 14 cm borehole was drilled from the surface.

At a depth of 812 m, the drill entered an open space. A camera was lowered.

The chamber measured approximately 6 m across. No entrance was visible.

A human body was lying against the eastern wall.

Foundation personnel enlarged the borehole and recovered the remains robotically.

The deceased was identified as D-1184.

DNA comparison produced a match to archived samples at all tested loci. Fingerprint comparison was positive. The body was wearing Foundation clothing issued during Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-12.

Cause of death was dehydration. Injuries to the hands were consistent with prolonged attempts to excavate rock manually.

Time of death was estimated at nine to thirteen days after Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-12.

D-1184 had remained in Foundation custody for six years following the experiment before dying of unrelated causes in 2010.

The recovered body was therefore designated D-1184-B.

No passage connects the chamber to the remainder of {{SCP_ID}}.

During autopsy, a stainless-steel orthopedic screw was discovered in the proximal phalanx of D-1184-B's left index finger.

The implant bore serial number:

████-██-11841

Foundation medical records show that D-1184 fractured his left index finger during an altercation at Site-██ on 11/19/2007.

The screw was implanted the following morning, three years and six months after D-1184-B's estimated death.

The serial number of the implant recovered from D-1184-B is identical to the implant removed from D-1184 during his 2010 autopsy.

Both implants are currently held at Site-██.
