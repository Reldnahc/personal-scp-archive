Special Containment Procedures

The incorporated municipality of ██████, Pennsylvania, is to remain publicly accessible and administratively intact. Foundation personnel embedded within county and state agencies are to monitor municipal elections, zoning actions, school enrollment, business closures, property transfers, annexation petitions, emergency evacuations, and other events capable of substantially altering ██████’s population, tax base, infrastructure, or legal status.

No Foundation employee assigned to {{SCP_ID}} may own or lease residential property, sleep, or establish a recurring overnight presence within ██████. Personnel may enter ██████ on no more than three consecutive calendar days before spending a minimum of eighteen continuous hours outside the municipal boundary. Analysis and recommendations concerning {{SCP_ID}} are to be drafted and submitted only after this period has elapsed.

Personnel who resist a scheduled rotation, characterize their departure as unnecessary, or request permanent reassignment to ██████ are to be removed without further discussion and evaluated at Site-41.

No Foundation action affecting more than twenty permanent residents, ██████’s municipal boundaries or charter, or the continued operation of a major employer, school, public utility, or medical facility may be authorized solely on the recommendation of personnel currently inside ██████.

Residents transported outside ██████ for medical treatment, criminal detention, or emergency evacuation are to be privately asked, after eighteen continuous hours outside the municipal boundary, whether they wish to return. Refusal is to be honored unless return is required to prevent an immediate threat to life. Former residents are not to be asked, encouraged, or induced to re-enter ██████ for testing purposes.

Any proposal to dissolve, annex, depopulate, or substantially reorganize ██████ requires Level-4 authorization and Ethics Committee review.

Description

{{SCP_ID}} is an anomalous decision-making process associated with the incorporated municipality of ██████, Pennsylvania, population 2,814.

Permanent residents of ██████ display normal variation in personality, occupation, religion, political affiliation, interpersonal relationships, and personal preference. No abnormal agreement has been observed regarding matters without a substantial effect on the municipality as a whole.

When ██████ is presented with a decision capable of significantly altering its population, economic independence, municipal boundaries, essential institutions, or continued existence as a distinct community, this variation progressively resolves into consensus.

Consensus is not immediate. Residents may disagree publicly, form opposing organizations, circulate petitions, retain legal counsel, or express incompatible personal interests. Over periods ranging from several hours to approximately three weeks, these disagreements diminish until one position is accepted by nearly all affected residents.

Individuals undergoing this process do not report intrusive thoughts, confusion, compulsion, or loss of agency. They remain capable of explaining their decisions in detail and generally regard their eventual position as the product of ordinary reflection.

These explanations are frequently incompatible with one another. Contradictory information may alter the reasons residents provide without altering the position they ultimately adopt.

Every confirmed consensus produced by {{SCP_ID}} has favored the continued functioning of ██████ or its preservation as an economically and administratively distinct community. This does not necessarily correspond to the outcome most beneficial to individual residents.

Following approximately six to twelve continuous hours outside ██████, affected individuals commonly reject decisions they previously supported. Many describe their former reasoning as excessive, irrational, or difficult to reconstruct. Re-entry into ██████ can restore the previous position within several minutes.

No corresponding alteration of autobiographical memory has been detected. Individuals generally remember both positions and describe both as sincere.

Permanent residency is not strictly required for susceptibility. Similar effects have occurred among individuals who established a prolonged working and domestic routine within ██████. See Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.3.

Discovery

{{SCP_ID}} came to Foundation attention in 2018 during a Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection investigation into groundwater contamination originating from ██████ Alloy Works, the town’s largest private employer.

Concentrations of trichloroethylene exceeding state limits were detected in forty-one residential wells. Temporary housing was offered to 163 residents while remediation was conducted.

All 163 refused.

The refusal was initially attributed to concern over property values, distrust of state officials, and fear that a prolonged evacuation would result in the closure of ██████ Alloy Works. Investigators nevertheless noted that several residents with young children declined free housing despite having previously submitted written complaints concerning the contamination.

Elaine Foster was transported to a hospital outside ██████ after developing symptoms consistent with solvent exposure. Foster had rejected relocation assistance three times during the preceding week and had described the proposed evacuation as “completely unnecessary.”

Approximately nine hours after leaving ██████, Foster contacted her husband and instructed him to bring her clothing, financial records, and identification documents to the hospital. When informed that she would be discharged the following morning, Foster stated that she would not return home.

During a recorded telephone conversation, her husband reminded her that she had refused relocation less than twenty-four hours earlier.

Foster responded:

“I know I said that. I don’t know why I said it.”

Two additional hospitalized residents demonstrated similar reversals during the following month. Foundation investigation began shortly afterward.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.1 — ██████ Quarry Expansion

Historical review identified several probable manifestations of {{SCP_ID}} predating Foundation discovery. The most significant occurred in 1996.

██████ Limestone Company, then the municipality’s largest employer, announced that its existing quarry would become economically nonviable within five years unless extraction was expanded eastward. The proposed expansion required the purchase and demolition of fourteen privately owned homes.

All fourteen homeowners initially opposed the proposal. Nine retained legal counsel, and two organized a petition demanding that the municipal zoning board deny the company’s request.

Over the following eighteen days, opposition declined.

At the final zoning hearing, all fourteen affected homeowners publicly endorsed the expansion. Surviving recordings show several speaking on behalf of ██████ Limestone Company and describing the demolition of their homes as necessary for the future of the town.

The zoning request passed unanimously.

All fourteen properties were purchased, and their occupants relocated outside ██████. Foundation personnel later located twenty-two surviving members of the affected households. Nineteen agreed to interview.

None continued to support the quarry expansion.

Six described the sale as the worst financial decision they had made. Four stated that they could not explain why they had agreed to it. Three denied ever supporting the proposal until shown recordings of their testimony.

Former resident Margaret Sayer was shown footage in which she urged the zoning board to approve the demolition of her home because “the town can’t lose another employer.” Sayer requested that the recording be stopped.

The following exchange occurred afterward.

Dr. Ellison: Do you remember saying that?

Sayer: Yes.

Dr. Ellison: Did you believe it?

Sayer: I must have.

Dr. Ellison: Do you believe it now?

Sayer: No.

Dr. Ellison: What changed?

Sayer: I left.

Sayer subsequently declined a Foundation request to visit ██████.

When asked why, she stated:

“I don’t want to find out that I agree with myself again.”

No further requests for former residents to re-enter ██████ were authorized.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.2 — Controlled Proposal

In 2020, three demographically matched groups of ██████ residents were asked to evaluate a proposed closure of ██████ Elementary School.

Group A was informed that closure would substantially reduce property taxes. Group B was informed that closure would substantially increase property taxes. Group C was informed that closure would have no effect on taxation. All three groups were told that students would instead attend a larger school approximately 18 km outside ██████.

Initial support for closure ranged from 38% to 61%.

Eleven days later, opposition measured 94%, 96%, and 93% among the three groups.

Participants provided different explanations corresponding to the information they had received. Members of Group A emphasized the importance of preserving a local institution despite potential savings. Members of Group B cited the financial burden of closure. Members of Group C cited travel time, community identity, and the effect on young families.

Participants were then informed that the financial reports had been fabricated.

No significant change in opinion occurred.

The false information altered the explanations residents produced. It did not alter the decision.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.3 — Municipal Dissolution Exercise

Before the adoption of current rotation procedures, the {{SCP_ID}} project maintained a field office in a rented commercial property inside ██████. Residential properties were also leased for personnel assigned to long-term observation.

On 7 June 2022, Regional Command authorized a closed planning exercise intended to evaluate whether a temporary evacuation and suspension of ██████’s municipal charter could isolate or terminate {{SCP_ID}}.

The exercise was not disclosed to any ██████ resident, municipal employee, or civilian official. Only eight members of the Foundation field team received the proposal.

All eight had maintained a regular working and domestic routine within ██████ for periods ranging from nineteen to sixty-one consecutive days.

Before deployment, six of the eight had supported further investigation of temporary evacuation or municipal dissolution. One had opposed such measures on ethical grounds. One had recorded no position.

The personnel were instructed to prepare independent assessments and were prohibited from discussing the exercise with one another until their initial reports had been submitted.

Twelve days later, all eight recommended that ██████ remain populated and incorporated.

Their stated reasons were not consistent.

Dr. Naomi Ellison argued that {{SCP_ID}} might constitute an intelligent entity and that dissolving ██████ could amount to its destruction. Specialist Aaron Beck argued that {{SCP_ID}} was probably unrelated to ██████’s legal status and that suspending the charter would therefore accomplish nothing. Researcher David Hsu argued that relocating the population might disseminate the anomaly beyond ██████. Agent Caroline Stroud argued that evacuation would likely terminate {{SCP_ID}} but would create an unacceptable risk of public exposure.

Every report concluded that no evacuation or administrative change should be attempted.

The field team also submitted a unanimous request to postpone its scheduled rotation and extend the leases on its office and residential properties. The request stated that replacing personnel would create “an unnecessary interruption in project continuity.”

Regional Director Morrow ordered the immediate withdrawal of all eight personnel.

Each objected to the order for a different reason. All eight were removed from ██████ under security escort.

Six hours after departure, five continued to defend their reports. After ten hours, two did. After fourteen hours, all eight formally withdrew their recommendations and requested that the dissolution exercise be reconsidered.

The following interview was conducted with Dr. Ellison eighteen hours after her removal.

Director Morrow: Do you still believe what you wrote?

Dr. Ellison: I believe every sentence in that report can be defended.

Director Morrow: Do you still recommend that we leave ██████ as it is?

Dr. Ellison: No.

Director Morrow: Why not?

Dr. Ellison: Because the sentences weren’t leading me to the conclusion.

Director Morrow: Explain.

Dr. Ellison: The conclusion was selecting the sentences.

Director Morrow: Did you feel compelled?

Dr. Ellison: No. I felt careful. Responsible.

Director Morrow: Responsible to whom?

Dr. Ellison does not respond for eleven seconds.

Dr. Ellison: At the time, I would have said the residents.

Director Morrow: And now?

Dr. Ellison: ██████.

During withdrawal, an encrypted storage module containing untransmitted project data was inadvertently left inside the field office. The module remained secured to a storage mount requiring Specialist Beck’s biometric authorization and could not be released remotely. Beck was therefore authorized to retrieve it twenty-nine hours after his removal. At the time, the effect of re-entry on nonresident personnel had not been established.

Beck entered ██████ accompanied by two security officers. Before entry, he affirmed that the field office should be closed and that no Foundation personnel should continue living inside the municipality.

Four minutes after crossing the boundary, Beck asked why the office was being abandoned. He was instructed to complete the retrieval and leave immediately. At nine minutes, having detached the module, he began reconnecting equipment that had been prepared for transport. At thirteen minutes, he explicitly refused a second direct order to leave and stated that the project’s removal was “a disproportionate response to an internal disagreement.”

Security personnel removed Beck from ██████ and recovered the module.

Nine hours later, Beck again endorsed closure of the field office. He retained an uninterrupted memory of both decisions.

When asked which position had been genuine, Beck responded:

“I wasn’t pretending either time.”

No further re-entry testing has been authorized.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.4 — Current Working Model

Early Foundation documentation described {{SCP_ID}} as a compulsive effect causing ██████ residents to make decisions favorable to the municipality.

This language has been removed.

No common compulsion has been identified. Residents do not receive identical thoughts, experience identical emotions, or construct identical rationales. Their individual decision-making processes remain observably distinct.

Only the outcome converges.

Following the municipal dissolution exercise, Dr. Ellison submitted the following memorandum:

We have been treating ██████ as a place that influences the people living inside it.

That may be the wrong scale.

A brain does not persuade its neurons to agree. Their competing activity is the process by which the brain reaches a decision.

██████’s arguments, campaigns, hearings, private doubts, and changes of opinion may not represent resistance to {{SCP_ID}}. They may be {{SCP_ID}}.

The residents may not be the subjects.

They may be the parts.

No method has been identified to determine whether {{SCP_ID}} possesses consciousness, self-awareness, or any capacity to act independently of individuals currently present within ██████.

After the municipal dissolution exercise, Regional Command ordered a complete review of {{SCP_ID}} containment by a committee whose members had never entered the municipality. The committee retained the requirement that ██████ remain populated and administratively intact. It also rejected a proposed twenty-four-hour evacuation of the entire population.

The committee was instructed to disregard the field team’s conclusions and evaluate only the underlying evidence.

No member of the committee had entered ██████.

Every document in the committee’s briefing packet had been selected, labeled, or summarized within ██████ by members of the former field team.
