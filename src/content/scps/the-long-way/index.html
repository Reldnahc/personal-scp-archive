Special Containment Procedures

{{SCP_ID}} is contained within Provisional Site-184, constructed around its original location near ███████, Virginia. The Foundation has acquired the surrounding property through a holding company. Civilian access is prohibited under the cover of groundwater contamination discovered during commercial development.

The structure in which {{SCP_ID}} was discovered has been demolished. Testing Chamber 184-1 has been constructed around the anomaly’s fixed coordinates.

The position and longitudinal axis of {{SCP_ID}} are to remain continuously marked by optical reference equipment mounted outside the anomalous interval. No permanent structure, conduit, cable, pipe, or other obstruction is permitted within 2 m of either endpoint.

No material is to be introduced along the anomalous axis without authorization from the project lead.

No portion of a human body is to be introduced into {{SCP_ID}}.

Description

{{SCP_ID}} is a spatial anomaly occupying an externally measurable interval 22.4 cm in length and approximately 3.8 cm in diameter.

Objects crossing this interval normally traverse 22.4 cm.

Objects entering the interval along a narrow range of trajectories approximately parallel to its longitudinal axis may instead traverse an anomalous path measuring 82,614.7 km before reaching the opposite endpoint.

Both distances are independently reproducible.

{{SCP_ID}} does not contain a three-dimensional interior volume as presently understood. Once an object has fully entered the anomalous path, measurable displacement is possible only along the direction of travel or its inverse. No perpendicular direction has been detected.

Rotation remains measurable relative to the object itself, but does not expose additional directions of travel or observation.

Objects entering {{SCP_ID}} retain momentum. An object allowed to continue without external force will traverse the anomalous path and emerge from the opposite endpoint after a duration consistent with its velocity and the 82,614.7 km internal distance.

Objects may also be withdrawn through their point of entry before full traversal.

Measurements obtained at different positions along {{SCP_ID}} vary in a manner corresponding to locations in conventional space extending away from Earth, passing around it, and returning toward the anomaly’s terrestrial location.

These locations are designated equivalent positions.

Objects within {{SCP_ID}} do not appear to physically occupy their equivalent positions. No reciprocal physical interaction between an object inside {{SCP_ID}} and matter at an equivalent position has been demonstrated.

The location of the 82,614.7 km comprising {{SCP_ID}} is unknown.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.1 — Discovery

{{SCP_ID}} was discovered on 14 September 2019 during renovation of a vacant commercial building near ███████, Virginia.

A construction crew was installing electrical conduit through a 22 cm reinforced-concrete partition.

After approximately 30 cm of penetration without breakthrough, the operator withdrew the drill and inspected the opposite side of the wall. No damage was visible.

The wall thickness was verified from the exposed upper edge of the partition.

A second hole, drilled approximately 15 cm from the first, penetrated the wall normally.

The operator then returned to the original position using a 1.0 m masonry bit.

The entire usable length of the bit entered the wall without emerging from the opposite side.

The bit was withdrawn intact.

No cavity or obstruction was identified.

Work in the immediate area was suspended. The site foreman reported the discrepancy to the property owner as a possible structural defect.

A consulting engineer inspected the wall the following morning. An inspection probe inserted through the original bore could be advanced beyond the measured thickness of the partition. Attempts made several centimeters to either side encountered the opposite surface normally.

The engineer’s report noted that the observed depth could not be reconciled with the wall, surrounding structure, or architectural plans.

The report was subsequently flagged by Foundation monitoring.

Foundation personnel assumed control of the property under the pretext of investigating subsurface contamination.

Initial testing focused on the concrete partition.

Radiography, ultrasonic inspection, and destructive sampling revealed no unusual structure.

Sections of concrete surrounding the bore were progressively removed.

{{SCP_ID}} remained in place.

The entire partition was subsequently demolished.

The anomaly persisted in the open space formerly occupied by the wall.

Removal of the surrounding floor, ceiling, and adjacent structural material produced no measurable change.

The Foundation acquired the property and adjacent undeveloped land in November 2019.

Provisional Site-184 was subsequently constructed around {{SCP_ID}}.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.2 — Characterization

Testing following demolition of the original structure established that {{SCP_ID}} remained fixed relative to the surrounding terrain.

No visible boundary could be identified.

Objects moved through the affected region perpendicular to its primary axis behaved normally.

A steel rod inserted parallel to the original construction bore could be advanced beyond the 22.4 cm separating the anomaly’s endpoints.

When the rod was withdrawn, its full length was recovered without alteration.

A laser directed across {{SCP_ID}} outside the anomalous trajectory was detected after the expected light-travel interval.

When aligned with the anomalous trajectory, the same pulse was detected at the opposite endpoint approximately 0.276 seconds after emission.

Repeated trials produced consistent results.

At the speed of light in vacuum, the measured delay corresponds to a path approximately 82,700 km in length.

Subsequent measurements refined this value to 82,614.7 km.

The source and detector remained 22.4 cm apart throughout testing.

To determine whether the additional distance applied only to electromagnetic radiation, a 500 m instrument cable was introduced along the anomalous trajectory.

The cable could be continuously fed into {{SCP_ID}} without emerging from the opposite endpoint.

Electrical continuity with the inserted portion remained intact.

The cable was subsequently withdrawn without damage.

No further tethered distance trials were authorized.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.3 — Dimensional Testing

Initial project documentation described {{SCP_ID}} as an elongated volume of space contained within the 22.4 cm interval.

This terminology was discontinued following dimensional testing.

Small instrument packages were introduced between 5 m and 400 m into {{SCP_ID}} while attached to retrieval lines.

Each package contained inertial sensors and independently actuated internal masses intended to produce rotation and lateral movement.

Rotation occurred normally.

No lateral displacement was detected.

Thrusters oriented along the direction of entry produced acceleration along {{SCP_ID}}.

Identical thrusters oriented perpendicular to that direction produced measurable force on the instrument package but no corresponding displacement.

No collision or resistance was detected.

Following perpendicular thrust, the package remained at the same measurable position along {{SCP_ID}}.

Cameras were subsequently tested at multiple orientations.

A camera directed toward either endpoint could detect light entering {{SCP_ID}} from that direction when sufficiently close to it.

Cameras oriented perpendicular to the anomalous axis recorded no incident light.

Rotation of a camera through 360° produced two observable directions separated by 180°. All intermediate orientations recorded no external scene.

For this reason, references to the “walls,” “width,” or “interior” of {{SCP_ID}} are considered inaccurate.

The 3.8 cm external diameter describes the region through which an object may enter the anomaly. It does not describe the dimensions available after entry.

Dr. Elena Varga proposed describing {{SCP_ID}} as a one-dimensional spatial path embedded between two points in three-dimensional space.

This terminology is used descriptively and does not represent a confirmed model of the anomaly.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.4 — Autonomous Traversal

Following measurement of {{SCP_ID}}’s approximate length, Project {{SCP_NUMBER}} personnel constructed an autonomous instrument package capable of completing the predicted traversal without a physical tether.

Probe {{SCP_NUMBER}}-1 contained synchronized clocks, inertial sensors, gravity-gradient sensors, magnetometers, radiation detectors, radio receivers, accelerometers, onboard data storage, and redundant power systems.

The probe was accelerated into {{SCP_ID}} at 102.4 m/s.

Under conventional geometry, the probe would have crossed the 22.4 cm interval in approximately 0.002 seconds.

It did not emerge.

Recovery equipment remained active at the opposite endpoint.

Probe {{SCP_NUMBER}}-1 emerged 9 days, 8 hours, 5 minutes, and 14 seconds later.

Its measured velocity upon emergence differed from its entry velocity by less than 0.2%.

The probe was recovered without significant damage.

Its internal clock agreed with Site-184 timekeeping within experimental tolerance.

Recorded data indicated continuous forward motion throughout the traversal.

No interruption, discontinuity, or period of unrecorded time was identified.

The total distance calculated from onboard inertial measurements was consistent with the value obtained through light-delay testing.

Analysis of the remaining instrumentation produced unexpected results.

During the first portion of traversal, measurements of the local gravitational field weakened.

The observed change was broadly consistent with increasing distance from Earth.

The local geomagnetic field weakened concurrently.

Radiation measurements also changed in a manner consistent with increasing altitude.

At greater internal distances, Probe {{SCP_NUMBER}}-1 received terrestrial and satellite radio transmissions at strengths and Doppler shifts inconsistent with reception at Site-184.

Using known satellite transmissions as reference points, analysts calculated a series of apparent locations for the probe.

These locations formed a continuous trajectory extending away from Earth.

Because Probe {{SCP_NUMBER}}-1 could not transmit data externally while inside {{SCP_ID}}, these calculations were performed after recovery.

The inferred locations were designated equivalent positions.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.5 — Equivalent Position

Additional autonomous probes were launched at lower velocities to permit more detailed measurements.

Data from these traversals established that equivalent position changes continuously with distance traveled through {{SCP_ID}}.

The inferred path initially extends away from the terrestrial surface.

It subsequently curves relative to Earth, reaches a maximum equivalent distance of approximately 34,000 km, and begins approaching the planet again from a different direction.

Near the end of traversal, equivalent positions converge on the geographic location of Site-184.

At 82,614.7 km, the probe emerges from {{SCP_ID}}’s opposite endpoint, 22.4 cm from its point of entry.

The relationship is sufficiently consistent that the approximate radio environment at a given internal distance can be predicted before a probe is launched.

This allowed a direct test of whether objects within {{SCP_ID}} physically occupy their equivalent positions.

On 3 February 2023, Foundation satellite FSV-12 was maneuvered through coordinates corresponding to an instrument package held stationary 41,208 km into {{SCP_ID}}.

The package was introduced using an autonomous braking system and remained at the predicted internal distance for twelve minutes.

During closest approach, the package received FSV-12’s telemetry beacon.

Signal strength was consistent with a separation of less than 400 m.

FSV-12 detected no transmission from the package.

No visual contact was established.

The satellite passed directly through the coordinates corresponding to the package’s calculated equivalent position.

Neither object recorded a collision or other physical interaction.

The instrument package was subsequently accelerated forward and recovered from the opposite endpoint.

Following this experiment, the hypothesis that {{SCP_ID}} passes through conventional outer space was rejected.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.6 — Current Model

No accepted physical model accounts for all observed properties of {{SCP_ID}}.

The anomalous path has a stable measurable length of 82,614.7 km and no detectable perpendicular spatial extent. Objects traversing it experience continuous passage of time and retain momentum.

Measurements obtained along the path correspond predictably to locations in conventional space, but no object within {{SCP_ID}} has been shown to physically occupy or interact with matter at those locations.

The endpoints remain 22.4 cm apart.

Following review of Probe {{SCP_NUMBER}}-4 data, the Foundation Department of Applied Physics recommended against referring to {{SCP_ID}} as a portal, wormhole, or spatial shortcut.

The recommendation was adopted.

{{SCP_ID}} does not shorten the distance between its endpoints.
