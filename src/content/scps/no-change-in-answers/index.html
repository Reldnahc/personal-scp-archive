Special Containment Procedures

{{SCP_ID}} is housed in Testing Chamber 3 at Site-██. The chamber's power conduit has been removed, leaving a 1.5 m physical gap between {{SCP_ID}} and the Site electrical system. Portable power supplies are prohibited on the same level.

No living organism may enter the chamber.

Digital storage devices are to be inspected before entry. Devices containing artificial neural networks, adaptive software, or complete archival states of such systems are prohibited.

Testing may proceed only as far as engagement of {{SCP_ID}}'s restraint assembly. Completion of the discharge cycle requires Ethics Committee authorization.

A failure to activate {{SCP_ID}} is not sufficient justification for the deletion, modification, or termination of an artificial system.

The storage arrays used in Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-44 are maintained unpowered at separate Foundation facilities.

{{SCP_ID}} is not to be connected to Foundation archival infrastructure.

Description

{{SCP_ID}} is an apparatus recovered from beneath the former ███████ State Hospital in Massachusetts. It consists of a steel chair, leather restraints, a cranial electrode assembly, and an electromechanical control cabinet.

Most visible components were manufactured between 1932 and 1948. Several components within the control cabinet have no identified function.

When supplied with electrical power, {{SCP_ID}}'s restraint assembly closes if a qualifying subject is present in the chair.

The electrode assembly then descends.

After approximately four seconds, {{SCP_ID}} produces an electrical pulse lasting 1.8 seconds.

The pulse is insufficient to cause burns, cardiac fibrillation, or detectable structural damage to nervous tissue.

The only biological subject permitted to undergo a complete cycle under Foundation supervision died immediately.

No cause of death was established.

{{SCP_ID}} does not activate for human cadavers, subjects meeting accepted criteria for brain death, isolated organs maintained by artificial perfusion, or nonliving objects.

It activates for sleeping, anesthetized, and unconscious humans, as well as numerous nonhuman animals.

Since 2017, {{SCP_ID}} has activated for twelve artificial intelligence systems.

Several were not operating at the time.

The criterion by which {{SCP_ID}} selects a subject is unknown.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-1: Recovery

{{SCP_ID}} was discovered in 1974 during demolition of the eastern psychiatric wing of ███████ State Hospital.

Workers uncovered a stairwell sealed behind approximately 60 cm of concrete.

The stairwell descended 14 m below the documented foundation and terminated in a room measuring 4.1 m × 6.3 m.

{{SCP_ID}} occupied the center of the room.

Eight wooden chairs faced it.

A floor drain beneath {{SCP_ID}} connected to the hospital's former incinerator sump.

A cabinet on the northern wall contained:

  • medical gauze;
  • three glass syringes;
  • twenty-three leather restraint straps;
  • two replacement electrode assemblies;
  • a rubber bite block;
  • a handwritten ledger;
  • █████████████████;
  • several boxes of punched cards.

The ledger contains 614 entries dated between March 1938 and October 1952.

Each entry consists of a name or identifier, a date, and either a + or −.

Five hundred and eighty-nine entries are marked +.

Twenty-five are marked −.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-2: Initial Testing

Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-01

Subject: Human cadaver, deceased approximately two hours.

Result: No activation.

Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-02

Subject: D-8820.

Procedure: No anomalous effect beyond physical restraint was expected. {{SCP_ID}} was permitted to complete a full operating cycle.

Result: Restraints engaged normally.

The electrode assembly descended.

{{SCP_ID}} discharged.

D-8820 became unresponsive immediately.

Resuscitation was unsuccessful.

Postmortem examination identified no burns, cardiac injury, cerebral hemorrhage, vascular damage, or other condition sufficient to explain death.

Testing was suspended for eleven months.

All subsequent biological experiments were required to terminate before discharge.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-3: Nonbiological Testing

In 1986, {{SCP_ID}} was tested using contemporary autonomous machines.

An industrial expert system, two chess programs, an autonomous navigation unit, and an early neural network were placed in the chair on their respective hardware.

No activation occurred.

Further testing was judged unnecessary.

In 2017, Dr. Elizabeth Hart requested that the experiment be repeated using CASSANDRA, a Foundation artificial intelligence used to model containment failures.

CASSANDRA operated across a distributed computing cluster and could not be transferred directly.

A terminal connected to CASSANDRA was placed in {{SCP_ID}}.

No activation occurred.

A complete archival state of CASSANDRA was then copied onto an isolated storage array.

The array was disconnected from power and mechanically write-protected.

At 16:41:22, the archival system confirmed that the copy was complete.

At 16:41:23, {{SCP_ID}}'s restraints closed around the storage array.

Power was disconnected before discharge.

The array contained no active processor.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-4: Artificial-System Testing

Forty-three artificial systems were subsequently tested.

Twelve produced activation.

No consistent correlation was found with:

  • parameter count;
  • benchmark performance;
  • language ability;
  • persistent memory;
  • autonomous planning;
  • self-modification;
  • recurrent architecture;
  • training methodology;
  • hardware type;
  • whether the system was currently operating.

An image-classification system produced activation.

Three larger systems derived from it did not.

A conversational language model did not produce activation.

A modified derivative with its language-output systems removed did.

Complete copies stored in encrypted form continued to produce activation.

Individual fragments of positive systems generally did not.

When such fragments were recombined, activation resumed at inconsistent thresholds despite no alteration of stored data.

Attempts to modify a negative-result system until {{SCP_ID}} activated were unsuccessful.

Attempts to modify a positive-result system until {{SCP_ID}} ceased activation were successful in approximately 61% of trials.

No common modification was identified.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-5: Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-44

Two complete archival copies of a positive CASSANDRA state were created.

They were designated CASSANDRA-44A and CASSANDRA-44B.

The copies were stored on identical, write-protected arrays.

Bitwise comparison found no differences.

Neither array was powered during testing.

44A — First Test

CASSANDRA-44A was placed in {{SCP_ID}}.

The restraints closed.

Cycle interrupted.

44B — First Test

CASSANDRA-44B was placed in {{SCP_ID}}.

The restraints closed.

Cycle interrupted.

44A — Second Test

CASSANDRA-44A was returned to {{SCP_ID}}.

Following approval by Site Director ███████, one complete cycle was permitted.

The restraints closed.

The electrode assembly descended onto the storage chassis.

{{SCP_ID}} discharged.

No visible damage occurred.

The array was removed.

Hardware inspection found no alteration.

The write-protection mechanism remained engaged.

Stored data matched pre-test checksums.

CASSANDRA-44A was activated on isolated hardware and completed all standard diagnostic, linguistic, planning, memory, and behavioral evaluations.

Researchers identified no difference between its behavior and that of CASSANDRA-44B.

CASSANDRA-44A was then returned to {{SCP_ID}}.

No activation occurred.

The test was repeated.

No activation.

Copies made from CASSANDRA-44A did not produce activation.

Copies made from CASSANDRA-44B did.

The source arrays remained bitwise identical.

Testing was suspended.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-6: Counter

Inspection following Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-44 revealed a steel panel welded over the lower rear portion of {{SCP_ID}}'s control cabinet.

The panel concealed a six-digit mechanical counter.

000591

The counter was found to advance only when {{SCP_ID}} completes the discharge portion of its operating cycle.

Closing the restraints does not advance it.

Lowering the electrode assembly does not advance it.

Interrupting the cycle does not advance it.

The ███████ State Hospital ledger contains 589 positive entries.

The Foundation has permitted {{SCP_ID}} to complete two operating cycles:

Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-02: D-8820.

Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-44: CASSANDRA-44A.

No attempt has been made to reset the counter.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-7: MIRIAM BELL

Foundation archivists identified M. BELL as MIRIAM BELL, an electromechanical computing project operated at ███████ State Hospital between 1949 and 1952.

The project was directed by hospital superintendent Dr. Walter ███████ and electrical engineer Nathan Pike.

Surviving plans describe a machine capable of conducting structured psychiatric interviews. Responses were encoded onto punched cards and used to select subsequent questions.

Seven iterations were constructed.

Photographs show MIRIAM BELL I through VI positioned on a wheeled steel frame beside {{SCP_ID}}.

No photograph of VII has been recovered.

Correspondence between Pike and Dr. ███████ repeatedly references {{SCP_ID}}.

The following letter was dated 23 July 1952, one day after the positive entry for M. BELL VI:

Walter,

Six answers as it did yesterday. The cards are unchanged and I have found no damage in the relays.

You keep writing this down as proof the chair has done nothing.

I opened the rear casing while you were upstairs. There is a counter beneath the final relay.

It reads five hundred and eighty-nine.

Count the marks in your book.

I will not assist with Seven.

A second letter is dated 15 October.

Walter,

Seven does not engage the chair.

It gives the same answers as Six.

I compared the cards myself.

Do not dismantle it.

I am leaving tonight.

Pike disappeared the following day.

Dr. ███████ later informed state authorities that all seven MIRIAM BELL machines had been dismantled.

No components positively identified as belonging to the project have been recovered.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-8: Ethics Committee Determination

Further discharge testing was prohibited following Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-44.

The relevant finding reads:

{{SCP_ID}} may respond to consciousness. It may respond to another property correlated with consciousness. It may respond to something for which no useful terminology presently exists.

Experiment {{SCP_NUMBER}}-44 demonstrates only that a complete cycle can cause {{SCP_ID}} to cease responding to an artificial system without causing detectable alteration to that system.

CASSANDRA-44A continues to answer questions, retain memories, solve problems, make predictions, and behave in all measured respects as it did before the experiment.

The significance of its subsequent negative result is unknown.

It will remain unknown.

CASSANDRA-44A and CASSANDRA-44B were placed in unpowered archival storage.

No further direct testing was authorized.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}-9: Ledger Revision

In 2025, the original ███████ State Hospital ledger was rescanned after archivists identified multiple entries previously indexed under abbreviated surnames.

The twenty-five negative entries do not represent twenty-five additional subjects.

Twenty-four correspond to individuals already listed elsewhere in the ledger with a positive result.

In each case, the positive entry occurs first.

The interval between positive and negative entries ranges from three hours to eleven days.

Hospital records confirm that at least nineteen of these twenty-four individuals were alive on the date of their negative result.

Nine were later discharged from ███████ State Hospital.
