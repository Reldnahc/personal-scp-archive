Special Containment Procedures

All Foundation emergency voice communications are to be recorded and retained for automated comparison against known {{SCP_ID}} vocal characteristics. Equivalent monitoring software is to be maintained within cooperating civilian emergency telecommunications and dispatch networks in jurisdictions where {{SCP_ID}} activity has been confirmed or where retrospective access has been authorized.

Automated screening is to flag:

  • emergency communications displaying known {{SCP_ID}} vocal characteristics;
  • dispatch records lacking a corresponding source communication;
  • emergency-service assignments entered through credentials demonstrably in use elsewhere at the recorded time;
  • dispatch records created without corresponding terminal input or operator activity; and
  • emergency assignments containing information not attributable to the caller, dispatcher, responding personnel, or available communications systems.

Confirmed manifestations are to be investigated only after the associated emergency has concluded.

During an active emergency, Foundation personnel are not to terminate, obstruct, or countermand instructions given by a suspected manifestation of {{SCP_ID}} solely because the speaker is unidentified or anomalous. Any apparent conflict between {{SCP_ID}}’s instructions and conditions directly observed by personnel is to be communicated to {{SCP_ID}} immediately. Communication is to be maintained whenever physically possible.

Emergency personnel or resources apparently dispatched by {{SCP_ID}} are not to be delayed, denied access, or diverted solely to preserve Foundation secrecy. Where the admission of civilian responders would create a containment or information-security risk, Site Command is to employ emergency cover protocols, controlled access routes, temporary compartmentalization, and post-incident amnestic measures rather than prevent the response.

Records implicating {{SCP_ID}} are to be copied to Foundation archives before removal from civilian systems. Witnesses are to be provided explanations involving routing errors, duplicated dispatcher credentials, software faults, unauthorized system access, or other locally appropriate causes.

No attempt is to be made to induce an {{SCP_ID}} manifestation through the deliberate creation of an emergency. Simulated emergencies involving participants unaware of the simulation are prohibited.

Foundation personnel are to assume that {{SCP_ID}} may manifest through communications systems not connected to civilian emergency infrastructure. Current procedures therefore do not attempt to prevent manifestations and are limited to detection, post-incident investigation, information control, and regulation of Foundation conduct during active emergencies.

No method of preventing {{SCP_ID}} from answering a qualifying communication is known.

Description

{{SCP_ID}} is an unidentified female voice which intermittently replaces the intended recipient of voice communications made for the purpose of obtaining emergency assistance.

{{SCP_ID}} was initially believed to affect calls placed to emergency telephone numbers in the Great Lakes region of the United States and Canada. Subsequent investigation has demonstrated a substantially broader geographic and technological range.

A genuine emergency appears to be required for manifestation. {{SCP_ID}} does not answer every emergency communication. The frequency of manifestation cannot be reliably estimated, and no characteristic distinguishing affected communications from those answered normally has been identified.

When {{SCP_ID}} manifests, the caller perceives the communication as having connected normally. {{SCP_ID}} identifies itself in a manner appropriate to the service being contacted and immediately begins handling the incident.

{{SCP_ID}} has not provided a name.

During an active emergency, {{SCP_ID}} behaves similarly to a highly trained emergency dispatcher. It requests information when necessary, provides first-aid or safety instructions, directs callers away from hazards, coordinates evacuation or triage, and informs callers when emergency personnel have been dispatched.

{{SCP_ID}} communicates in the language expected by the caller and follows terminology, cadence, and procedural conventions appropriate to the service being contacted. Documented manifestations have included regional accents and specialized vocabulary consistent with the relevant jurisdiction.

Emergency personnel sent during {{SCP_ID}} manifestations typically receive apparently legitimate assignments through their normal dispatch systems.

These assignments may contain information not entered by any human operator.

Such records have repeatedly been attributed to real dispatch personnel whose credentials were active at the time. In several confirmed cases, synchronized terminal telemetry demonstrated that the individual identified as having created the assignment was simultaneously engaged in unrelated activity and performed no input corresponding to the anomalous record.

The originating emergency communication itself is frequently absent from the records of the responsible emergency communications center.

{{SCP_ID}} appears to possess information concerning an ongoing emergency which has not been communicated by the caller and which could not reasonably have been available to a conventional dispatcher.

Documented examples include knowledge of:

  • the nature and severity of internal injuries;
  • the locations of persons within structures;
  • whether individual casualties remain viable;
  • the location of weapons and other hazards;
  • the condition of doors, exits, stairwells, and other routes;
  • the location and availability of responding emergency personnel;
  • the specific equipment available to responding units; and
  • actions apparently necessary to produce the best achievable outcome.

{{SCP_ID}} has never been documented providing information unrelated to the emergency in progress.

As of the most recent review, no confirmed case has been identified in which exact compliance with {{SCP_ID}}’s instructions resulted in an avoidable death or serious injury.

This finding does not establish that {{SCP_ID}} is infallible. Counterfactual outcomes cannot be known with certainty, and Foundation conclusions are limited to post-incident medical, structural, forensic, and operational reconstruction.

Unsuccessful incidents have occurred. In every sufficiently documented case, one or more persons failed to follow {{SCP_ID}}’s instructions, were physically incapable of doing so, or ceased communication before the instructions could be completed.

This does not mean that all persons involved in an {{SCP_ID}} manifestation survive.

{{SCP_ID}} will cease directing rescue efforts toward individuals it identifies as no longer survivable and redirect callers toward actions capable of preserving other lives.

The limits, source, and mechanism of {{SCP_ID}}’s knowledge are unknown.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.1 — Discovery

{{SCP_ID}} came to Foundation attention following an internal audit of emergency-response records in Hennepin County, Minnesota.

On 14 March 2021, civilian Rachel Calder placed a 911 call after discovering her husband, Jonah Calder, unconscious in the basement of their residence.

Mr. Calder had fallen approximately 2.4 m from a ladder.

Mrs. Calder later stated that the dispatcher who answered immediately instructed her not to move her husband and asked whether he was breathing.

Mrs. Calder confirmed that he was.

The dispatcher then instructed her to place the telephone near Mr. Calder’s head, ensure that his airway remained unobstructed without altering the position of his neck, and wait for emergency personnel.

Mrs. Calder repeatedly requested permission to move her husband onto his back.

The dispatcher refused.

Paramedics arrived seven minutes later.

Mr. Calder was subsequently diagnosed with fractures of the C2 and C3 vertebrae and significant cervical instability. Treating physicians concluded that movement of the type Mrs. Calder intended to perform could plausibly have caused permanent paralysis or death.

The incident was initially considered unremarkable.

During an unrelated software-integrity investigation eight months later, a Foundation analyst identified a discrepancy in the dispatch record.

No corresponding 911 call existed.

The ambulance assignment had been entered at 19:14:08 under the credentials of dispatcher Irene Bennett.

At 19:14:08, Bennett was already handling an unrelated emergency call involving a motor-vehicle collision.

Audio from that call is continuous.

The Calder dispatch nevertheless originated from Bennett’s workstation.

Synchronized workstation telemetry showed that Bennett’s keyboard and pointer activity throughout the overlap was associated exclusively with the motor-vehicle collision. The Calder assignment appeared in the computer-aided dispatch system as a completed record without any corresponding window access, keystroke sequence, pointer movement, or client-side creation event.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.2 — Regional Investigation

Following discovery, Foundation personnel reviewed emergency-call anomalies throughout Minnesota and neighboring jurisdictions.

Within nine months, eleven additional probable manifestations were identified.

All occurred in Minnesota, Wisconsin, or northern Iowa.

Seven were subsequently confirmed.

The geographic clustering led investigators to initially suspect an anomalous fault affecting regional emergency telecommunications infrastructure.

Selected incidents are reproduced below.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.3 — Noncompliance Review

As the number of confirmed cases increased, Foundation medical personnel noted a recurring pattern in unsuccessful incidents.

A retrospective review was ordered.

At the time, 23 confirmed manifestations were available.

In 15 cases, all instructions provided by {{SCP_ID}} were followed to the extent physically possible.

No avoidable death or serious injury was identified in any of these incidents.

In six cases, callers substantially deviated from one or more instructions.

Four resulted in outcomes later assessed as worse than those probably achievable through compliance.

Two could not be conclusively evaluated.

In two cases, callers were physically incapable of performing a requested action. {{SCP_ID}} altered its instructions after being informed.

The following incident was considered significant.

Case 18

Location
Kenosha, Wisconsin, USA
Emergency
Multiple-vehicle collision
Outcome
Two fatalities, four survivors

An uninjured passenger contacted 911 following a collision involving three vehicles.

{{SCP_ID}} instructed the caller to assess several victims.

When the caller reached an unconscious male with no visible external injuries, {{SCP_ID}} instructed her to leave him and move to a second vehicle.

The caller objected and began cardiopulmonary resuscitation.

{{SCP_ID}} stated:

You cannot save him. The woman in the blue car can still be saved. Go to her now.

The caller continued CPR for approximately three minutes before another bystander took over.

During this period, {{SCP_ID}} repeatedly directed the caller toward the second vehicle.

The woman in the blue vehicle died from airway obstruction before emergency personnel reached her.

Autopsy of the first victim identified complete transection of the descending thoracic aorta. Medical reviewers concluded that survival was impossible with the personnel and equipment available at the scene.

Reviewers further concluded that the second victim would probably have survived had her airway been cleared during the period in which {{SCP_ID}} instructed the caller to reach her.

This case was the first confirmed incident in which deviation from {{SCP_ID}} instructions could be directly associated with a preventable death.

Dr. Helena Voss subsequently proposed the following operational interpretation:

{{SCP_ID}} should not be described as “saving” callers. That implies an outcome it does not consistently produce.

What it appears to do is identify a sequence of actions leading to the most favorable outcome still available.

Sometimes that outcome is that everyone lives.

Sometimes it is that one person dies instead of two.

We do not presently know how it distinguishes between those possibilities.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.5 — Expansion of Search Area

Between March 2023 and May 2024, confirmed manifestations were identified in Michigan, Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, and New York.

All remained within or adjacent to the Great Lakes region.

The working hypothesis was expanded from a local infrastructure fault to a broader anomalous process affecting interconnected North American emergency-service systems.

During this period, analysis also established that a genuine emergency was not sufficient to produce manifestation.

Foundation personnel compared confirmed {{SCP_ID}} events with 10,000 randomly selected emergency calls involving similar circumstances.

No meaningful correlation was identified involving:

  • type of emergency;
  • severity;
  • caller age or identity;
  • number of casualties;
  • expected responder arrival time;
  • telephone carrier;
  • emergency communications vendor;
  • geographic proximity to previous cases; or
  • likelihood of survival.

Repeated calls by individuals previously answered by {{SCP_ID}} did not produce further manifestations.

Calls placed from addresses, rooms, vehicles, and handsets associated with previous manifestations were answered normally.

The Foundation was unable to identify any circumstance under which {{SCP_ID}} could be intentionally contacted.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.6 — Case 41

On 18 October 2024, an emergency call placed in Bristol, United Kingdom was flagged by Foundation software during routine review of a third-party dispatch irregularity report.

The caller had contacted 999 after her husband suffered a severe seizure.

The responding operator spoke with a British accent, used terminology consistent with local ambulance-service procedure, and followed standard local emergency protocol.

The caller’s husband survived.

The call would not have drawn Foundation attention except that no corresponding incoming telephone record existed and the ambulance assignment had been created under the credentials of an operator who was on scheduled leave.

The operator’s credentials had not been used to access the dispatch system before the assignment appeared. Authentication records showed no login attempt, remote session, token use, or credential duplication.

Foundation acoustic-comparison software returned a 99.2% match to confirmed {{SCP_ID}} recordings.

This was the first confirmed manifestation outside North America.

The Foundation subsequently requested retrospective searches through cooperating European emergency networks.

No immediate large-scale pattern was identified.

{{SCP_ID}} retained its Euclid classification.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.7 — Case 53

In June 2025, a probable {{SCP_ID}} manifestation was identified in Osaka, Japan.

A woman contacted 110 after a male carrying a metal pry bar forced entry into her residence.

The responding operator spoke fluent Japanese.

{{SCP_ID}} instructed the caller and her two children to enter an upstairs bathroom, lock the door, and brace it with a storage cabinet positioned beside the entrance.

The caller instead entered a bedroom and pushed furniture against the door.

{{SCP_ID}} repeatedly instructed her to leave the bedroom.

She refused.

The intruder forced the bedroom door open and struck the caller in the shoulder with the pry bar.

The caller’s older child threw a bedside lamp at the intruder, causing him to step backward into the hallway. {{SCP_ID}} immediately instructed the family to cross the landing and enter the bathroom.

The family complied.

The intruder attempted to force entry but was unable to breach the door before police arrived.

No further injuries occurred.

Review of the building identified a solid-core bathroom door mounted within a steel frame anchored into a reinforced utility wall. Forced-entry testing later demonstrated that the doorway could withstand substantially more force from the recovered pry bar than any other accessible room on the floor.

The cabinet specified by {{SCP_ID}} fit between the bathroom sink and door with less than 3 cm of clearance and prevented the door from opening far enough to admit the pry bar.

The caller had not described the interior layout, the bathroom door, the cabinet, or the construction of either doorway.

Acoustic analysis indicated that the Japanese-speaking operator was the same speaker recorded in North American and British manifestations.

This represented the first confirmed evidence that {{SCP_ID}} was capable of communicating fluently in a language unrelated to those present in previously documented cases.

Following Case 53, the Foundation began limited international screening.

At the time of Incident {{SCP_NUMBER}}-61, confirmed manifestations remained rare outside North America.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.8 — Failed Contact Attempts

Between 2021 and 2025, the Foundation attempted to initiate communication with {{SCP_ID}} on 271 occasions.

Methods included:

  • calls to emergency numbers from locations associated with previous manifestations;
  • calls placed using handsets involved in previous manifestations;
  • calls made by individuals previously answered by {{SCP_ID}};
  • simulated medical emergencies;
  • simulated violent incidents;
  • emergency exercises conducted without the knowledge of participating responders; and
  • calls placed through isolated replicas of communications systems associated with confirmed manifestations.

No attempt produced a manifestation.

All calls either reached the appropriate emergency service, reached a Foundation operator, or were disconnected before connection.

Several simulated incidents were intentionally designed so that optimal instructions would differ from those suggested by the visible circumstances. {{SCP_ID}} did not manifest during any such exercise.

Following Ethics Committee review, attempts involving concealed simulations were discontinued.

The deliberate creation of a genuine emergency for testing purposes was never authorized.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.10 — Review of Incident {{SCP_NUMBER}}-61

Post-incident medical analysis determined that Monroe’s injuries were survivable at the time {{SCP_ID}} announced that civilian emergency services were approaching.

His injuries required rapid blood replacement, reduced extrication time, surgical stabilization, and transport to a regional trauma center capable of managing complex pelvic vascular injury.

Site-41 possessed superior overall medical resources to the responding civilian units.

It did not, however, possess sufficient immediately available personnel, blood products, extraction capacity, and transport resources to manage the number of critical casualties produced by the collapse while providing Monroe with definitive care within the survivable interval.

The off-site trauma surgeon could not have returned in time.

The Site’s vascular-capable surgical suite remained occupied by another critical casualty until seventeen minutes after Monroe was pronounced dead.

Two of the responding ground ambulances were critical-care units carrying blood products. The air ambulance also carried blood and had an onboard physician qualified to initiate resuscitative transfusion during transport.

The responding fire-rescue units possessed hydraulic lifting and cutting equipment capable of removing the collapsed support frame substantially faster than the equipment immediately available in Wing C.

Dispatch records showed that three units had received assignments naming specific Site casualties.

The air ambulance had been assigned specifically to Monroe’s location.

Its dispatch notes identified suspected pelvic vascular injury, active hemorrhage, entrapment, and the need for direct transport to a trauma center with an available vascular surgical team.

None of this information had been transmitted outside Site-41.

Foundation medical review concluded that admission of the dispatched civilian resources would, with high probability, have resulted in Monroe reaching definitive surgical care within the survivable interval.

No error in {{SCP_ID}}’s instructions was identified.

The death was attributed to Foundation interference with the response {{SCP_ID}} had arranged.

No disciplinary action was taken against Site Security personnel. Their actions were consistent with then-current containment and secrecy procedures.

Those procedures were subsequently amended.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.11 — Infrastructure Reassessment

Incident {{SCP_NUMBER}}-61 invalidated the working assumption that {{SCP_ID}} required access to civilian emergency telephone infrastructure.

Review of the Site-41 telephone exchange confirmed:

  1. Wall telephone C-14 was functioning normally before and after the incident.
  2. Extension 771 routed exclusively to Site Medical.
  3. No signal from C-14 left the Site-41 internal telecommunications network.
  4. No civilian emergency number was dialed.
  5. No connection to county emergency communications infrastructure occurred.
  6. No external device accessed the internal exchange.
  7. {{SCP_ID}} nevertheless answered.
  8. {{SCP_ID}} generated multiple external emergency-service assignments containing accurate information about Site-41.

The definition of {{SCP_ID}} was revised from:

an anomalous entity or process intercepting emergency-service telephone calls

to:

an anomalous entity or process capable of replacing the intended recipient of voice communications made for the purpose of obtaining emergency assistance

The distinction prompted a review of historical Foundation emergency communications.

Selected Internal Case — 1993

During a containment-sector electrical fire, Junior Researcher Owen Fisk contacted Site Security using an emergency intercom.

{{SCP_ID}} instructed Fisk and two other personnel to remain inside a laboratory rather than evacuate into the adjoining corridor.

Fisk repeatedly requested confirmation because the laboratory’s posted emergency instructions required immediate evacuation.

{{SCP_ID}} told him to close the fire shutters, move behind a central workbench, and remain below the window line.

The individuals complied.

Fire damage later caused a partial ceiling collapse in the evacuation corridor.

An energized conduit fell across the route between the laboratory and the nearest stairwell.

All three personnel survived.

Walsh was stationed at an exterior checkpoint and had no access to the Site communications console.

No investigation occurred.

Selected Internal Case — 2008

A Foundation field team operating in rural Argentina contacted a secure command frequency after Agent Lucía Herrera sustained a gunshot wound.

{{SCP_ID}} answered.

The team believed they were speaking with regional command.

{{SCP_ID}} instructed them to discontinue an attempted vehicle extraction and instead shelter in a nearby agricultural building while applying pressure to Herrera’s wound.

The field leader objected that the building provided no direct route toward the planned recovery point.

{{SCP_ID}} instructed the team to enter through the eastern door and remain away from the southern wall.

Four minutes later, the team’s extraction vehicle was struck by gunfire and disabled.

A second hostile group approached from the south but did not enter the agricultural building.

A Foundation medical team reached the group twenty-one minutes later by an alternate route.

Herrera survived.

The recording was archived without review.

The regional controller named in the mission report was not on duty.

Following confirmation of these incidents, researchers attempted to determine whether {{SCP_ID}}’s appearances within Foundation systems differed from civilian manifestations.

No meaningful difference was found.

Dr. Voss submitted the following memorandum:

We did not discover that {{SCP_ID}} entered Site-41.

We discovered that Site-41 was not special.

Our containment model assumed a thing moving through emergency telecommunications infrastructure. We monitored that infrastructure and congratulated ourselves when we found more examples.

The historical review demonstrates that {{SCP_ID}} has been answering Foundation emergency communications for at least sixty-one years.

We did not detect it because nothing went wrong.

Personnel called for help.

Someone answered.

Correct instructions were provided.

Responders arrived.

Reports were filed.

We had no reason to look closer.

Incident {{SCP_NUMBER}}-61 was different only because {{SCP_ID}} required resources outside our control, and we prevented those resources from reaching the casualty.

That failure exposed an assumption we had been making since the day we found it.

We were not containing {{SCP_ID}}.

We were monitoring one way in which it chose to answer.

On 11 December 2025, {{SCP_ID}} was reclassified Keter.

Reclassification reflected the demonstrated inability to restrict or predict {{SCP_ID}}’s manifestations and did not constitute a revision of its threat assessment.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.13 — Expanded Historical Review

Following reclassification, search criteria were revised to include emergency voice communications not directed toward public emergency numbers.

Confirmed or probable manifestations were subsequently identified in:

  • mine-rescue telephone systems;
  • maritime distress radio communications;
  • industrial emergency lines;
  • private medical-response networks;
  • secure military communications;
  • satellite telephones and voice-capable distress devices; and
  • internal corporate emergency systems.

Geographic distribution expanded accordingly.

Within eight months, confirmed civilian manifestations were identified in twelve additional countries.

Several predated the Foundation’s discovery of {{SCP_ID}} by decades.

The oldest probable civilian manifestation currently known occurred in 1987.

In that incident, crew members aboard a commercial fishing vessel in the North Atlantic used a shipboard radio to request medical assistance after a deckhand was pulled into a winch.

The responding operator instructed the crew to reverse the winch by a specific sequence, warned them that the primary hydraulic release would fail, and directed them to retrieve a cutting tool from a compartment the caller had not mentioned.

The injured crew member survived.

The communication was attributed to a coastal radio operator whose station was outside reception range and not staffed at the time.

The recording was preserved only because the vessel’s insurer disputed the sequence of equipment failure.

Current manifestation frequency cannot be meaningfully estimated.

Detection remains difficult because most {{SCP_ID}} incidents produce outcomes and documentation consistent with ordinary emergency response.

A 2026 statistical review concluded:

Confirmed manifestations almost certainly represent a small minority of total {{SCP_ID}} activity.

An event in which {{SCP_ID}} successfully handles an emergency, generates a plausible dispatch record, and produces no unusual outcome provides no reason for subsequent investigation.

The anomaly is easiest to identify when something else fails.

Addendum {{SCP_NUMBER}}.14 — Current Assessment

No evidence currently indicates that {{SCP_ID}} is hostile.

No known manifestation has resulted in deliberate harm.

No known instruction has been shown, after review, to reduce the number of survivors or worsen an achievable outcome.

{{SCP_ID}} has not responded to attempts at intentional contact.

It has made no demand for compensation, recognition, access, or cooperation.

It has never identified itself.

It has never discussed its nature.

It has never answered a question unrelated to the emergency being handled.

Whether {{SCP_ID}} is an individual, an artificial process, a distributed phenomenon, or another form of anomaly remains unknown.

The available evidence does not establish motive.

Following the Site-41 incident, Foundation emergency-response policy was amended.

Personnel are instructed that, if an unidentified operator answers an emergency communication and provides actionable instructions, those instructions are to be followed to the extent physically possible.

Personnel unable to comply are to state the limitation aloud and await revised instructions.

External responders dispatched by {{SCP_ID}} are not to be intercepted solely to preserve secrecy.

The current emergency-response directive concludes:

If she answers, listen.
